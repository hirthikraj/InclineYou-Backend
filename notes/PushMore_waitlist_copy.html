00Wordmark

The name, and the one rule about setting it

The product is Push More. It's an instruction, not a noun — which is the whole reason it works for this audience. Two words in prose, always with the space.

Wordmark

Push More

Push is lime. More is the same ink as the text around it. That contrast is the entire mark — don't colour both words, don't add a third colour, don't outline it.

#4D7C0F  lime on light
#A3E635  lime on dark
#16191A  ink
  • Two limes, one mark. The bright lime is illegible on a white page and the dark lime dies on a black one — pick per background, never one for both.
  • In running text the name is plain: "Push More does this." Only the wordmark, the logo and the nav get the lime split. Colouring the name mid-paragraph every time it appears turns the page into confetti.
  • Lime is the brand's only saturated colour. Everything else on the landing page stays neutral, or the mark stops being the loudest thing on screen.
  • Set solid or spaced — "PushMore" as one tight lockup, or "Push More" with the space? The deck assumes a space. Decide before the logo is drawn, because it changes the domain and the handle too.
  • Check the name is free: Play Store, App Store, a .com or .in, and the Instagram handle. "Push" is a crowded word — do this before any of it is printed.

02Problem

Name their evening back to them

Recognition before pitch. Nothing here is a feature — it's a trainer reading their own week and thinking "how do they know that." Keep it short enough that it never becomes a complaint.

You didn't get certified to do admin.

  • Someone asks if you're free at seven on Tuesday. You picture the week, say yes, and find out later you weren't.
  • A client asks what she lifted last Thursday. You scroll up four weeks of WhatsApp to find out.
  • Three people owe you this month. You're sure about two of the names.
  • You've rebuilt the same fat-loss program nine times, because the template only exists in your head.
  • The gym takes its cut and you work out your share on a calculator at the end of the month.
  • Your best client quit, and you had nothing to show him for eleven weeks of work.

None of that is training. It's the unpaid half of the job — and it's the half that makes clients leave.

  • Six lines is the ceiling. Seven starts to read as pity.
  • The scheduling line goes first on purpose — it's the one that happens daily and costs the least to admit to.
  • The last line is the hinge into the product. Give it air — its own line, not the tail of the list.
03Product

What Push More actually does

Eight blocks, in the order a trainer's day runs: plan → log → fill the day → move things around → prove it → get paid → split it → chase. Every claim here is something the app genuinely does today. Nothing is aspirational.

01

Build a program once. Reuse it forever.

Pull from a full exercise library — sets, reps, rest, target load. Save any program as a template and hand it to the next client in one tap, then tweak it for that person without touching the template or anybody else's plan.

Screenshot: program builder with a template being applied
02

Log on the floor. Signal or no signal.

Open the client, tap the set, move on. Last session's load and reps are already filled in, so most sets take two taps. Everything saves on your phone first and syncs itself when you get network back — so a basement gym, a terrace, or a dead spot costs you nothing.

Screenshot: set logger mid-session · this is the hero screenshot
03

See your day — and the free spots in it.

Your diary opens on today, scrolled to right now. Sessions in order, and between them the gaps, marked with the times you could actually book. Someone asks if you're free at seven — you look, instead of guessing.

Tell Push More your working hours once and it stops offering slots you don't work. Block out a wedding, a holiday, or a Thursday morning and those hours go quiet. Try to book a slot that's already taken and it says so, and offers you the next free one.

Screenshot: day view with an open gap expanded into bookable times · second hero screenshot
04

Set the week once. Move things without ten messages.

Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays at 7am for eight weeks — set it once and every session is on the calendar, with the dates shown to you before anything is booked.

When someone needs to move, change the time and the client is told for you. Mark a session done and it comes off their pack. Mark a no-show and it's on record. And if yesterday's sessions are still sitting unmarked, Push More tells you the moment you open the app, so the month doesn't end in guesswork.

Screenshot: recurring-slot preview, and a session being rescheduled
05

Proof your client can see.

Every set you log builds their chart. Personal bests get spotted on their own. Once a week each client gets a progress report — what they lifted, what they beat, how many sessions they turned up for — sent automatically, in your name.

Screenshot: progress chart + a weekly report as the client receives it
06

Get paid, straight into your UPI.

Send a link that opens your client's UPI app with your ID and the amount already filled in. The money goes from them to you — Push More never holds it and takes no commission. Session packs count themselves down, and you can see who's due before they go quiet on you.

Screenshot: payment due chip → UPI app opening
07

The gym's cut, already calculated.

Set the split per client, not per trainer — because most of us are on a gym's floor and taking private clients at the same time. Your dashboard shows this month's revenue with your share and the gym's share separated for you.

Screenshot: dashboard revenue split
08

Reminders that go where your clients already are.

Session tomorrow. Payment due. Plan expiring. One tap sends it on WhatsApp, or let Push More send it for you. Nobody has to install anything to hear from you.

Screenshot: WhatsApp reminder composer with a prefilled message

06The maths

What it's worth, and how we make ours

Two blocks that have to sit together. The first turns the product into rupees, so the price in the next section reads as small. The second says plainly how Push More earns — because every app that has ever touched an Indian trainer's money took a percentage, and saying our incentive out loud is worth more than another feature.

What Push More is worth to you

It costs less than one session. It only has to save you one.

  • One gap filled a week. Your diary shows you the openings in today. Fill one at [₹600] and that's [₹2,400] a month you weren't earning before.
  • One payment that stops slipping. Most trainers are carrying two or three late fees at any moment. Collecting one of them on time, every month, pays for Push More several times over.
  • One client who stays a month longer. Clients who can see themselves getting stronger renew. One extra month on a [₹4,000] package is worth more than a year of the app.
  • One no-show you saw coming. A reminder the evening before costs you a tap and saves you an empty hour.
  • The evening back. Two hours a week of spreadsheets and scrolling for old numbers, at whatever your hour is worth.

We're not going to promise you more clients — we don't find you clients, and anyone who says they will is selling something else. What Push More does is stop you losing the clients and the rupees you already have.

How we make money

One flat fee a month. Nothing else, from anyone.

  • No commission on your fees. Money goes from your client's UPI app to yours. It never passes through us, so there is nothing for us to take a cut of — not now, not when we're bigger.
  • Your clients pay nothing. The client app is free and stays free. We don't want your clients to be a cost you have to justify.
  • No ads, and we don't sell your data. You pay us, so you're the customer. That's the whole arrangement.
  • Cancel and take your data with you. Your roster and your history export out. We'd rather earn next month than trap you in it.

We're telling you this before you sign up rather than after, because you've been burned by the other version.

  • These two blocks are one unit — value, then how we're paid. Splitting them across a scroll break loses the effect.
  • Set the rupee figures as the largest numbers on the page after the headline. This is the section a trainer does arithmetic in.
  • No graphs, no ROI calculator, no slider. A five-line list a trainer can check in their head is more persuasive than a widget they don't trust.
  • If the page runs long, this section can move below the offer — but it can never be cut. It's what makes the price feel like nothing.
  • Every rupee figure here must come from a real design-partner trainer's actual session rate and package price, not from our heads. An inflated number is spotted instantly by the only audience that matters.
  • "Cancel and take your data with you" promises an export. Confirm we'll have one, or cut that line — it's the easiest promise here to accidentally break.
  • Decide whether the monthly price appears on this page at all, or only after they join. Naming it is more honest and filters better; hiding it gets more signups. Recommended: name it.

08Trust

The four fears, answered before they're asked

A trainer's client list is their entire business. Every one of these is a thought they'll have on this page whether we address it or not. Short, flat, no hedging.

  • We take no cut of your fees. Payments go from your client to your UPI ID. The money never passes through us, so there's nothing for us to take.
  • We're not a marketplace. Your clients will never be shown another trainer. We don't sell training — we sell you a tool.
  • Your roster is yours. It lives in your account, not your gym's. If you change gyms, it moves with you.
  • We keep almost nothing. A name, a phone number, and training numbers. No medical or health information, ever. Built to India's DPDP Act — ask us to delete your data and it's gone.

Made in Chennai, with trainers in Chennai.

  • Plain block, no shields or lock icons. Security theatre reads as something to hide.
  • "Your roster is yours" is the strongest line here for gym-employed trainers. Consider pulling it up first if the ground game is gym-led.
09FAQ

Questions they'll actually ask

These came off the objections a trainer raises in a two-minute gym-floor demo. Answer in their words, not ours. Nothing here should need a second read.

When do I get it?

We're onboarding trainers in small batches from [month], Chennai and Coimbatore first. You'll get a WhatsApp message when your batch opens.

What will it cost?

Free while you're a founding trainer. After that, one flat monthly fee — less than what you charge for a single session. No commission on your payments, ever.

Do my clients have to pay or install anything?

No. The client app is free, and clients who don't want an app still get their reminders and reports on WhatsApp, the way they do now.

I'm employed at a gym and I also take private clients. Which one is this for?

Both, at the same time. Gym-collected or you-collected is a setting on each client, not on you. Your dashboard keeps the two straight.

Can my clients book their own sessions?

Not for now — you own your calendar. When a client wants to move something they'll message you like they always have; the difference is you move it in two taps and they're told automatically. We'll open up client booking when trainers tell us they want it, not before.

Does a no-show cost my client a session from their pack?

No. Only a session you mark done comes off the pack. A no-show is recorded so you both have the history, but it doesn't burn anything.

I train the same people every Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

Set it once — days, time, how long, how many weeks — and every session is on your calendar. You'll see all the dates before anything is booked.

Will it work on my phone?

Android and iPhone. It's built to run on a mid-range Android with a bad connection, because that's where the gym is.

I have 6 clients. I have 45 clients.

It's built for 10 to 30. Below that it still saves you the evening; well above that, join anyway and tell us — we want to hear from you before we build for it.

Is my client list safe if I leave the gym?

Yes. Your clients are attached to your account, not to any gym. Nobody at a gym can see or take them.

Tamil?

English at launch. Tamil is the first thing we add after.

Does it write programs for me?

No. You're the trainer — we're not going to pretend an app knows your client better than you do. What we do is make sure you never build the same program twice.

  • Accordion is fine, but the last two should be open by default — they're the ones that convert a hesitant gym-employed trainer.
  • Launch month.
  • Is the Tamil answer a commitment we're willing to be held to? It's currently scoped after launch, not in it. Soften to "Tamil is on the list" if that's shaky.
  • The no-show rule is still open internally. Today a no-show doesn't touch the pack, and the answer above says so — but plenty of trainers charge for no-shows and will read "doesn't burn anything" as us costing them money. Settle the rule before this page goes live, and rewrite the answer to match.
  • The client-booking answer promises we'll add it "when trainers ask". Only keep that sentence if we mean it.

12Voice

How everything on this page should sound

Rules for anything written later — ads, reels, the follow-up WhatsApp message — so it stays the same voice as the page.

Do

  • Short sentences. Read them aloud; if you run out of breath, cut.
  • Say "you". Never "trainers can now…"
  • Use their words: clients, sets, reps, session pack, split, PR.
  • Rupees, always. ₹, not $.
  • Concrete numbers over adjectives — "two taps", "30 clients", "no signal".
  • Say what the app doesn't do. It's the fastest way to be believed.

Don't

  • "Users", "leverage", "seamless", "empower", "elevate", "revolutionise", "game-changer", "10x".
  • "AI-powered". We don't do it, and trainers read it as "an app that thinks it knows better than me".
  • Any claim about growing their income. We don't get them clients and shouldn't imply we do.
  • Testimonials, trainer counts, or star ratings until they're real and attributed.
  • Streaks, leaderboards, gym-owner features, group or batch sessions, client self-booking, nutrition, wearables. None of it is in the first release — putting it on this page buys us churn.
  • Percentages of anything. The moment a trainer reads a % on a page about money, they assume we're taking one.
  • Talking down about spreadsheets. Their system got them here.
  • Punning on the name. "Push yourself", "push through", "no pain no gain" — the name already does that work, and doing it twice makes it a slogan instead of a product.
  • No social proof exists yet. Every gap on this page for a testimonial, a trainer count, or a logo stays empty until a real design partner says a real sentence on the record. Placeholder proof is the one thing that will cost us this audience permanently.
Sections run in the order a trainer reads them. Cut from the bottom, never the top — the page still works if it ends at 07.
