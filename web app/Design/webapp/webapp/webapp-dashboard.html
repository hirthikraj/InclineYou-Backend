Today — a to-do list, not a report

The page this replaces opened with Good morning, Anbu and four month-to-date money cards. The app it documents ships app/src/home/deck.ts, whose docstring settles both in one sentence: “a trainer’s home is a to-do list, not a report … No chart until the day is over, and no greeting at all.” The old deck was the exact inverse — the greeting the file rules out, in the position the file gives to what happens next, with money promoted from fourth of six modules to first of three. It was not under-designed. It was designed against a decision the product had already made, in writing, in a file that ships.

So this pass throws away the greeting and the stat row and asks the question the screen is named after. At 09:12 on this Tuesday a remote check-in has 18 minutes to run, the next session is 7 h 48 min away, 30 minutes of working time are left this morning — less than the hour a session needs — and the only stretch left long enough to sell starts at 19:00. None of that was on the old screen. What was on it, in the largest type available, was ₹1,06,500 under the word Billed, which is the month’s collected figure — making the trainer’s own number wrong by ₹18,000.

Nothing in the prose is hand-typed. The day, the working hours, the clock, the rate and the gym’s cut are imported from gen_schedule.py and the shell from gen_rail.py, so the date says Tuesday where the old page said Tuesday 12 August — a Wednesday.

SUPERSEDED · 23 Aug 2026 — the web app is ONLINE-ONLY

This page was written offline-first. That decision has been reversed for the web: the web app writes every response straight to the server — no browser storage, no write queue, no sync. Offline-first remains the architecture of the phone (app/) and of nothing else.

So wherever this page draws or argues for an offline banner, a synced / queued pill, a “held on this device” count, or the sync-queue screen, that chrome is not built on the web. The reasoning around it is kept, because the rest of each finding depends on it — only the offline half is void.

Authority: CLAUDE.md at the repo root, and web app/web/AGENTS.md. Do not re-derive the offline behaviour from this page.

01The audit — ten things, and the product had already decided four of them

Ten things, and the first four are one decision seen from four sides. The page this replaces was not under-designed. It was designed against a decision the product had already made, in writing, in a file that ships — the docstring at the top of app/src/home/deck.ts:

app/src/home/deck.ts · lines 9–12

“The order of the modules is the finding the teardown produced: a trainer’s home is a to-do list, not a report. What’s next, where the day stands, who needs chasing, the schedule, the money, what happened. No chart until the day is over, and no greeting at all.”

The old deck opened with Good morning, Anbu and four month-to-date money cards. 01The date is a Wednesday

Defect

What is wrong

“Tuesday 12 August” appears four times on the old page. In the 2026 calendar 12 August is a Wednesday. The whole design set had this bug and gen_schedule.py fixed it in its header comment — “in the actual 2026 calendar 1 August is a Saturday, so 11 August is a TUESDAY and all seven labels were off by one day” — and the one screen whose entire subject is a date was left out of the fix.

How to check it

python3 -c "import datetime; print(datetime.date(2026,8,12).strftime('%A'))". This page imports its day from gen_schedule: WEEK_MON + timedelta(days=TODAY) is Tuesday 11 August, and a date that is computed cannot be off by one. The import is asserted, not trusted.

02The headline money figure is under the wrong word

Defect

What is wrong

The old stat row led with ₹1,06,500 labelled Billed this month. On the money screen that figure is Collected; Billed is ₹1,24,500. DeckMoney in deck.ts has both as separate fields and derives yours: billed - cut, so the trainer’s own number is ₹75,500 and the old deck showed ₹57,500 — out by ₹18,000, which is the whole of the month’s remote work.

How to check it

Grep both files for the figures. And here is why nobody caught it: two of the money file’s numbers collide by accident. Collected (₹1,06,500) equals floor billing, and pending (₹18,000) equals remote billing. So collected − cut and floor − cut are the same subtraction, the arithmetic closed, and the wrong base was invisible. Both collisions are asserted in this script so the next edit to that file breaks the build instead of the page.

05The queue was in no order

Defect

What is wrong

buildAttention assigns every row a weight and sorts on it. Since the roster was drawn those weights are bands, one table in deck.ts read by both screens: money past its date, then an empty pack, then a quiet client, then a pack running low, then money not yet late. A band is worth 1000 and the amount inside it is clamped, so a big number orders rows within a band and can never lift one out of it. Sorting the old page’s own six rows by them moves 3 of the five. A sixth client — Vikram T, ₹3,000, 4 days late — was not on the list at all, though the stat row above it counted him. And two rows carried the wrong verb: the app says Remind for money and Nudge for silence, and the old page said Nudge for both.

How to check it

§07 puts the two orders side by side with the weights printed. The badge is the other half: it read 7 because the sync queue and the weekly reports were rows in it, and neither is an AttentionItem — AttentionKind is overdue | quiet | pack, three kinds. Sync is chrome and already a pill in the top bar. The badge is 6.

06The week card reported a finished week on the second morning of it

Defect

What is wrong

It read Sessions made 18 of 21 and 86% adherence. At 09:12 on Tuesday, 9 of the week’s 23 sessions had started. 18 sessions cannot have been made when 9 have begun. It also quoted an eight-week average of 84%, where the reports screen that owns the measure says 72%.

How to check it

02Competitor analysis — two families, and nobody in the middle

Six products, and the survey produced one finding rather than six: the category splits into two families and nobody is in the middle. The coaching products build an attention queue and no money. The booking and point-of-sale products build a money snapshot and no attention queue. X REP’s deck already tried to be both, which is the right ambition and was, in the old page, the wrong order — it led with the family the trainer opens once a month.

Rows marked Verified trace to the vendor’s own help centre or product page. Where a page returned 403 to a direct fetch, the row says which claims came from search summaries of that page rather than from the page itself.

TrueCoach — the dashboard IS the landing screen

Verified from vendor product page

What they built

Their dashboard page is explicit: it is the first thing you see when you log in, and it carries four named queues — Today’s Workouts, Reminders, Due Soon (a client’s program is running out) and Needs Attention (missed workouts, declining compliance, unusual patterns), plus client comments. Today’s workouts are sorted in-person, dual, then remote-only. The marketing claim is a “one-screen system”.

Take this

Everything. This is the closest thing in the category to what X REP is building and it validates three separate decisions: the home screen is a queue, the queue is named by what it wants from you rather than by which table it came from, and Due Soon is our PACK_ENDING rule under a better name. The in-person / remote sort is our floor / remote split, and it is first on their list too.

Leave this

Four separate queues. Four lists of one to three rows is four headings, four empty states and four places to look, and a trainer with one late payment and one empty pack has to scan all four to find two rows. Ours is one ranked queue with the kind stated in the row — which is what buildAttention’s weights already produce.

ABC Trainerize — the dashboard is about a client, not about your day

Partly verified · help centre returned 403

What they built

Their Client Insights Dashboard (early access) is a per-client surface: four widgets showing the most-viewed insights, and a colour-coded Weekly Compliance grid of habit, nutrition and workout completion. Clicking a widget opens the matching section of that client’s Progress tab. The trainer-side Things to do today that their help centre describes is a copy of the client’s calendar for the current day — again, one client at a time.

Take this

The compliance grid as a shape: seven cells, one per day, colour as the only encoding. It is the cheapest possible answer to “is this person still turning up” and it would sit in a 300px column. Worth considering for the client file rather than for Today.

Leave this

Per-client as the only scope. A dashboard you have to open a person to see is a report, not a deck. The trainer’s question at 09:12 is not “how is Meera doing” — it is “who is next and is anything about to go wrong”, and no per-client screen can answer it.

Everfit — a Today screen, in the client app

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Everfit ships a screen called Today and it is documented under Client App: the client’s own workouts, tasks and habits for the day. The coach side is organised around the client list and the Task Library; the habit-coaching insights dashboard is again per-client, a “quick glance at which clients are hitting their goals and which are falling behind”.

Take this

The name, and the fact that they gave it to the person who has to act today. Also the habit dashboard’s framing — hitting versus falling behind — which is a two-state ranking and exactly what severity: alert | critical already is.

Leave this

Giving the day to the client and the list to the coach. The coach has a day too. Ours is the trainer’s day, and the client portal is a separate, deliberately sparse file.

PTminder — money on the home screen, and it is the wrong money

Verified from vendor help centre

What they built

The dashboard shows total money made over the year, the previous month and the current month, plus pending invoices. The gym-versus-trainer split does exist — but as Payroll Reports, calculated from Pay Rates set per staff member, with report variants By Class, By Session, By Service, By Hours and By Salary, each ending in a column called Trainer Payout.

Take this

The confirmation, and the inversion. This is the only product in the survey that computes the number X REP is built on — and it computes it from the gym’s side, as a payroll line, for a business paying a member of staff. The trainer is the payee, not the user. X REP turns the same arithmetic the other way up: the trainer is the user and the gym is the deduction. That is one sentence, and it is the product.

Leave this

Year-to-date on a daily screen. Money made this year is a number a trainer looks at in March when deciding whether to leave the gym. It is not a number that changes what they do at 09:12, and a home screen that leads with it has decided the user is an owner.

03Nielsen Norman, and the nine decisions it settled

Nine principles, each with the change it actually caused. A principle that did not move a pixel is not listed — this is not a compliance checklist, it is the audit trail for nine decisions.

PrincipleWhat it says What it changed here
Vanity metrics
Harley, 2019		 A vanity metric “appears impressive but doesn’t give insight into the true performance”; a tracked metric should be actionable — changes in it should map to changes in the health of the thing. Perpetually-growing counts are the classic case. The four month cards went. Billed this month ₹1,06,500 · +8% on July is a monotonic count with a month-over-month delta, and on the 11th nothing the trainer does before lunch changes it. What replaced it in that position is 18 min left and starts in 7 h 48 min, both of which change every minute and both of which have an action attached.
Scrolling and attention
Fessenden, 2018		 120 participants, 130,000+ fixations: 57% of viewing time above the fold, 42% in the top 20% of the page, 74% within two screenfuls. Put high-priority content and the major call to action above the fold. Every frame here is exactly 900px of content, measured by rendering headless and scanning up for the last non-canvas pixel. The old page put 3 panels under 900 while claiming in the audit that nothing else was on the screen. The top 20% — 180px, which is the hero row almost exactly — now holds the only two things that change during the day.
Progressive disclosure Disclose up front only what is frequently needed; put the rest one gesture away. The five-row day table went. It listed the same five clients, in the same order, with the same times and states, as the rail three inches to its left — whose own source comment says the pins are “the same five the Today screen lists”. The ribbon shows what the rail cannot (shape, the hole, the sellable stretch, now); the rail shows what the ribbon cannot (names, times, ticks). Neither repeats the other.
8 guidelines for complex applications · 6
Kaplan, 2020		 Reduce clutter without reducing capability — staged disclosure, options shown when relevant. Two whole panels left and no capability did. Your own training is gated off in the app; Free time you could sell became one dashed block on the ribbon at the hour it refers to, which is both smaller and more useful than three rows of text about other days.
… · 7 Ease transition between primary and secondary information without leaving the screen. The ribbon’s blocks are buttons carrying an aria-label with the client, both times, the plan, the mode and the state — so the secondary detail is on hover and on focus, and the primary reading stays a shape.
… · 8 Make important information visually salient — by adding emphasis, or by removing what competes with it. Mostly by removing. The old screen had four 27px display figures in its top row, all about the month, competing with each other. There are now two 36px figures, and they are an elapsed clock and a start time.
Heuristic 1 · visibility of system status Keep the user informed about what is going on, through appropriate feedback within reasonable time. Three things that were implicit are now drawn: now, as a line through the day at 09:12; a session that started and was never opened, which the app can distinguish and the block ladder could not (see §09); and which hours are yours, as the two unhatched windows.
Heuristic 4 · consistency and standards Users should not have to wonder whether different words or situations mean the same thing. This one stopped a design. The month was going to become a five-figure strip across the bottom of the screen, and it is instead a card in the third column, the same shape as its two neighbours — because a screen whose layout changes on the 1st of the month teaches nothing twelve times a year. The CSS for the strip was written and then deleted; the comment where it lived says so.
Heuristic 8 · aesthetic and minimalist design Interfaces should not contain information which is irrelevant or rarely needed. The icon column in the queue went. At 15px in a table cell it rendered as what the stylesheet’s own comment calls “a smudge”, and it was encoding a category the row’s own words already state.

The one that cuts the other way

Heuristic 5, error prevention, argues against something on this screen. The queue’s six buttons — four Remind, two Renew — are one click from sending a real WhatsApp message to a real client about money. Nielsen’s fifth heuristic asks for a confirmation before actions “with serious consequences”, and this qualifies: the schedule file’s own rule is that a mis-drop which messages a client must be held for ten seconds with the countdown visible. The buttons here do not do that yet, because the hold belongs to the nudge component and not to this screen. It is §13’s first row rather than a footnote, because shipping this screen without it would make the most dangerous control on it the easiest one to press.

05The day, sideways — one minute, one pixel

The schedule file’s whole redesign was one sentence — one minute is one pixel — and it was drawn vertically, seven days across. Today needs the same geometry rotated: a block’s left is its start minute minus the ribbon’s, its width is its duration in minutes. No scale factor and no rounding, which is what makes the arithmetic below checkable with a ruler.

10:00 – 16:30 · between shifts, 6½ hours — not sellable
06:0008:0010:0012:0014:0016:3018:0020:3009:12

THE ARITHMETIC

06:00 to 20:30 is 870 minutes, so the track is 870px.

The content column at 1440 is 1144px wide. 870 fits inside it at 1:1 with 274px left over, which is the entire reason this can be a ribbon rather than a chart with an axis scale. At a wider canvas it does not stretch; it stays true and the column gets emptier.

THE FLOOR

A 30-minute session is 30px, and a 24px avatar fits in it.

Nothing else does. So the blocks carry the person and nothing else — no time, no plan name — and the shortest session this product books is exactly wide enough to be a face rather than a bar. The names, times and ticks are in the rail, which is already listing the same five.

WHAT IT REFUSES TO FOLD

6½ hours of the 870 minutes is the gap between two shifts, and it keeps every pixel.

The week grid folds that same span to a 30px seam, and is right to: across seven days it holds nothing. On one day it is the shape of the day. A trainer who works 06:00–10:00 and 16:30–20:30 is not busy from six till half eight, and a ribbon that compressed the middle would say they were.

The hatch means what it means on the week. Outside a working window the ground is the same 45° hatch .cw__off uses, for the same reason the schedule file gives: working hours constrain what a client can self-book and have never constrained the trainer, so the hours are hatched rather than blocked. The one dashed block is .gapb, unchanged from the week — a free interval inside working hours, long enough to sell, labelled with what it is worth. Today has exactly one: 19:00–20:30, 1 h 30, ₹800 billed and ₹432 kept. Two of the three the old page offered fell entirely outside the trainer’s windows, and the third overran its end by an hour.

Two things the render found that reading could not

The now chip covered the session it was pointing at. At 09:12 the marker sits 192px into the track, and Divya R’s block occupies 180–210 — so a 40px label at top:-1px covered the whole of the one block the frame exists to show. The week grid gets away with the identical trick because its blocks are 60px wide and the label sits in the last 40px of the row; a ribbon’s blocks are 30px wide and there is nowhere on them to put a label. The chip moved to the ruler, under the line, where nothing can be behind it.

And at 20:52 it floated past the end of the day. The evening frame’s clock is after the last window closes, so now − LO is 1132px in an 870px track: the line was clipped by the track’s overflow and the chip, which now lives on the ruler, was not — a label reading 20:52 sitting on top of the 20:00 tick. A day that is over has no now on it, so the marker is drawn only when the clock is inside [06:00, 20:30].

06One slot, five states

Hero.tsx opens with a sentence this design set had not acted on: “The top of the deck — the one thing on the screen that changes shape. Four states, one slot.” It exports five, and the reason the phone gives for the display figure is worth quoting whole, because it is the only reason in the file that does not survive the move to a desk:

app/src/screens/main/home/Hero.tsx

“The 62px display figure is the same in every state. It is the only element on the screen readable at arm’s length on a gym floor, which is the whole reason it is that size.”

A desk does not have arm’s length, so 62 became 36 — measured against the 27px stat value it is still unmistakably the largest thing on the page. But the other reason for a display figure survives intact: a trainer walks past this screen between clients, and a number they can read while walking is worth more than a row they have to stop for. What did not change is the rule that all five states use the same slot at the same size, so the shape of the screen never moves.

THE HOLD BELONGS TO THE VERB

4 of the 6 rows wait ten seconds. 2 are instant.

Remind and Nudge send a WhatsApp. Renew writes a package row locally and tells nobody, so it commits at once. A blanket delay on the whole column would make renewing a pack feel broken, which is what happens when a safety rule is applied to a table instead of to an action.

THE COLLISION, DRAWN ON PURPOSE

The held row is the critical one, so the accent tint and the danger edge have to share it.

This is the arrangement where this stylesheet’s opening rule could go wrong a second time — a lime ground swallowing a danger mark is exactly what the hours editor did. The countdown is .cw__bar, the schedule’s own.

11The keyboard, and a ribbon that is not a grid

This screen adds one new interactive surface — a horizontal track of positioned buttons — and the question it raises is the same one the week grid answered: is it a grid? The week’s answer was no, because its cells are gone and role="grid" promises a reader rows and columns it can count. A ribbon has neither, so it is what it looks like: a list of buttons in a landmark, in time order, each with a full accessible name.
