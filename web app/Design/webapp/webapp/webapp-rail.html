The rail — the one thing on every screen

It is drawn 31 times across this design set and specified once, and both numbers are a problem. Three of its rows contradict something else on the same screen, a feature that shipped on both halves has no route into it at all, and all 374 of its rows are <div>s that Tab walks straight past. This page re-specifies it: ten destinations grouped by how often they are opened, today’s clients in the rail itself, a 64px state that keeps the badge it used to delete, and an accelerator on every row. Two of the four changes come from a competitor who rebuilt their sidebar last year and published the reasoning; one of them is a refusal of what they did.

SUPERSEDED · 23 Aug 2026 — the web app is ONLINE-ONLY

This page was written offline-first. That decision has been reversed for the web: the web app writes every response straight to the server — no browser storage, no write queue, no sync. Offline-first remains the architecture of the phone (app/) and of nothing else.

So wherever this page draws or argues for an offline banner, a synced / queued pill, a “held on this device” count, or the sync-queue screen, that chrome is not built on the web. The reasoning around it is kept, because the rest of each finding depends on it — only the offline half is void.

Authority: CLAUDE.md at the repo root, and web app/web/AGENTS.md. Do not re-derive the offline behaviour from this page.

02The audit — six things wrong with the one thing on every screen

Six findings. Every one of them is checkable against a file in this directory, and the first three are visible in a frame that has already been reviewed — which is the argument for specifying furniture rather than pasting it.

Why the sync one is the worst of the six

The other five are things a trainer would never notice. This one is the product telling a trainer three different things about whether their work is safe, in one glance, on the screen they open first — and this is an offline-first product, where “has it reached the server” is the only question the chrome exists to answer.

The fix is not a better badge. It is one surface: the pill in the top bar carries the state and becomes the link when the queue is not empty, and Sync queue leaves the rail. Every frame on this page draws the pill as 6 queued, in .sync--queued — a variant that has existed in the stylesheet the whole time and has never once been used.

The day’s to-do list keeps its 6 entries waiting to sync row. A task list is allowed to repeat chrome state; repeating it is what a task list is for.

What did not move, and why that matters more

The four rows a trainer uses every day are the four rows they already know from the phone, in the same order, at the top. Nothing in this redesign is worth breaking that: a rail earns its keep by being in the same place tomorrow, and the fastest navigation in any product is the kind nobody reads.

64px, chosen and remembered. The count on Money is now a red dot rather than nothing — the rule it replaces was display:none, which threw away the only number on this rail that costs a trainer rupees, at exactly the width where a trainer has the least to look at. The tone survives the collapse; the digit does not, and the aria-label still says “3 clients owe you”.

Every glyph gets a tooltip on hover and on focus, at 140ms. The group kickers become hairlines, so the grouping survives too. The mark is the way back out — at 64px the brand row cannot hold a 28px mark and a 32px control, so one of them had to also be the other.

One token had to change to make the dot work. The accent dot is --tx-accent-text, not --tx-accent. Lime is a fill and a dot is a fill, so the rule technically allows it — but a 7px fill is competing with the surface rather than covering it, and #C6F24E on the light rail’s #EDEFEC measures 1.1:1. The text token is the lime on dark and #4F6B0A on light, which is what a mark the size of a full stop wants in both themes. Caught by rendering the collapsed rail in light, which is the second thing this frame existing has now found.

The refusal: it is not the default, and hover does not expand it

Everfit’s new sidebar is collapsed by default and expands on hover, with a Keep panel showing pin for people who want it open. It is a defensible trade and we are not taking it, for three reasons.

One. Nielsen Norman Group’s position on icon usability is that a text label must accompany an icon to disambiguate it, and that icon labels should be visible at all times, without any interaction from the user. Hover-to-reveal is interaction.

Two. Hover has no keyboard equivalent and no touch equivalent. A convertible laptop — the machine --w-tap-touch exists for — cannot hover at all.

Three. A panel that grows over the content when the pointer passes through it on the way to something else is a panel that moves without being asked. On a 1440px canvas we are not short of 184px; the collapse exists for the 1000–1200px window, and for the trainer who wants the width for a twelve-week grid.

So: expanded by default, collapsed by a control, remembered per device, and every glyph labelled by a tooltip that fires on focus as well as hover.

09Competitor analysis — six patterns, four changes

The existing market file examined five patterns and none of them was navigation — it went straight to the builder. These six are about the shell, and the first is the strongest single piece of evidence in either document, because it is a competitor rebuilding their sidebar and publishing the reasoning. Rows marked Verified trace to the vendor’s own release notes or help articles, which describe their interface in specifics. Where I could not verify a claim from a primary source, the row says so.

Everfit — the sidebar rebuilt around the client list

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Their release note New interface, navigation and color scheme describes a sidebar where the full client list is visible in the left sidebar, with a search field in the navigation panel, sortable alphabetically or by last app activity. And one detail worth the whole article: when navigating between different clients using the sidebar, the same tab you were viewing will remain open.

Take this

Objects belong in the rail, not just sections. A trainer’s day is five or six named people, and making them navigate Clients → search → open five times is the single largest avoidable cost in our shell. And tab persistence, taken verbatim: pin to pin, the sub-tab survives. Nothing in this design set does that yet.

Leave this

The whole list. 22 clients at our row height is 880px — taller than the rail, so it can only exist as a scroll that pushes the destinations out of view. Ours is cut to today, which is derived from the schedule and needs no sort control, no search field and no curating. The manual pin survives as the override.

Everfit — collapsed by default, labels on hover

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

The same sidebar is collapsible: at rest it shows a concise list of icons for a cleaner and more streamlined appearance, and labels appear on hover. A Keep panel showing control pins it open so that all features remain visible when you click on them.

Take this

The collapse, and remembering the choice. Both are right, and our 64px state already existed in the stylesheet — it had simply never been drawn, which is how it kept the bug in §02.4. The Keep panel showing label is also a good reminder that the state must persist per device rather than per session.

Leave this

Collapsed as the default, and hover as the reveal. NN/g’s position on icons is that a label must accompany the glyph and that icon labels should be visible at all times, without any interaction from the user. Hover is interaction, has no keyboard equivalent, and does not exist on the convertible laptop --w-tap-touch was measured for. We expand by default and collapse on request.

ABC Trainerize — a left menu, a top bar, and a library folder

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Their redesign shipped a new, easy-to-navigate menu on the left-hand side, and a top bar navigation (which means a few things have been relocated), plus search to find clients faster. The sidebar carries a Master Library folder holding Programs, Workouts, Habits and Exercises — a disclosure group inside the rail.

Take this

The split of a rail and a top bar, which is what we already have — and the grouping instinct: authoring surfaces belong together. Our BUILD group is the same idea. Their parenthetical is also the honest part of any nav redesign: a few things have been relocated, which is §10 on this page.

Leave this

The folder. A group you have to open is a group you cannot see, and it costs a click on every visit to save 60px once. Ten destinations do not need a tree; a kicker and a gap say the same thing for free and never hide anything.

The category — the navigation vocabulary is settled

Partly inferred

What they built

Read across the published help structures — TrueCoach’s help centre is organised as Managing Clients, The Workout Calendar & Sidebar, Library, Programs, Payments, Messaging, Transfers and Team Accounts, Coach Profile; Everfit’s left menu carries Clients, Library, Payment Activity, Studio — and the same eight nouns keep appearing.

Take this

Use the settled words. Clients, Schedule, Programs, Exercises, Reports, Team, Settings are all category vocabulary and none of them needed inventing. Two rows deliberately are not: Money, because Payments describes a processor and this is a book that also holds the gym’s cut, and Nudges, because they are outbound templates rather than a conversation.

Leave this

Messages. Every competitor has a chat row; we do not run a channel, and a Messages row would promise one. Nudges send over WhatsApp and email and the reply arrives in the trainer’s own inbox — which is honest, and a rail row named Messages would stop being honest about it.

The category — a sidebar that carries no state

Partly inferred

What they built

In the material I could reach, no competitor’s sidebar carries a number that changes the shape of a coach’s day. Where badges appear at all they are unread-message counts. There is no queue count, no unsent-work indicator, and no amount owed.

Take this

The gap. Three of our rows carry a number a trainer can act on, and the loudest of them — ₹18,000 owed across three clients — has no competitor equivalent at all. That is the same conclusion the market file reached about the money screen, arrived at from a completely different direction, which is the best kind of agreement.

Leave this

Treating the rail as a menu. A menu lists where you can go. A rail in an offline-first product also has to say what is waiting and what has not reached the server — and if it says the second thing, it has to agree with the top bar, which is the finding this page opens on.

Modern application shells — favourites, and a palette for the long tail

Pattern, widely documented

What they built

Outside this category the settled shape is: a short list of destinations, a group of pinned objects above or below them, and ⌘K carrying everything else. Linear, Notion, Slack and Attio all have a favourites group; none of them grows the menu to cover the long tail.

Take this

Both halves. The pins group is the first, and the second is a refusal: no search field in the rail. Everfit put one there, and it is the right call for a sidebar holding 200 names — but our top bar already has the omni box on ⌘K, and two search boxes on one screen is the same duplication as two sync indicators.

Leave this

Favourites above the destinations. Almost everyone puts them at the top. Ours go at the bottom, because our first four rows are the phone’s tab bar in the phone’s order, and a group whose length changes every morning must not be allowed to push them down the column.

OpenWhy it is not answered here
The empty pins group A rest day, or 06:00 on a Sunday. The group should disappear rather than say “no sessions” — a rail is furniture and furniture does not report — but that is a claim, not a drawn frame.
The seventh pin The scroll is specified and not drawn. A trainer with nine sessions is a real trainer, and a scrollbar appearing inside a 248px column is worth seeing before it ships.
Auto-collapse below 1200px Tempting, and refused for now: the design system draws the line at 1000px and says below it the phone app is the answer. A rail that collapses itself is also a rail that ignores the choice a trainer made, which is the complaint in §05.
A team lens in the foot Deliberately not invented. The app’s lens is 'trainer' | 'client' and nothing else. A “viewing X’s book” switch in the rail foot would be a permission model invented from a chevron.
The bell and the Nudges badge Two numbers about outbound messages, one in the top bar and one on the rail. They are not the same number — drafts waiting versus things that happened — but nothing in this design set says so, and a reviewer is right to ask.
Adherence has no row It is a section of Reports here, which is defensible. But the IA marks it PROMOTED and gives it its own frame, and a promoted screen reachable only by arriving somewhere else is worth a second look.
