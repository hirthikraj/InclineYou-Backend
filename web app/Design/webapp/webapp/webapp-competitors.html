Competitor analysis — what they built, and what is worth taking

Five interface patterns from the products a solo trainer would otherwise buy, each with the part worth stealing and the part worth refusing. One of them — Everfit’s week-by-week view — is a better idea than anything in X REP’s current builder, and this document says so.

Patterns examined 5Primary sources vendor product and help docsFindings to adopt 3Findings to refuse 3

Read this before the rest

Trainer-software comparisons published online are almost entirely pricing and feature matrices. I fetched several and found no usable interface detail in them — no layout, no navigation model, no interaction notes. So the concrete UI findings below come from the vendors’ own product and help documentation, which describes their interfaces in specifics. Where I could not verify a claim from a primary source, the row says so rather than guessing. A confident teardown of a screen I have not seen would be worth less than an honest gap.

01The patterns

Everfit — the three-pane builder

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Library on the left with filters by primary focus, category and video availability. Builder in the centre with drag-and-drop and section types (regular, interval, AMRAP, timed). Right pane does double duty: an arrangement panel giving a bird’s-eye view of the whole workout for fast reordering, and a schedule panel that shows other workouts on the calendar plus the client’s training history for comparison.

Take this

The history-beside-the-work idea, which X REP already does in the workout console’s Last time column — independent confirmation that it is the right call. And the arrangement panel: a bird’s-eye list for reordering is genuinely better than dragging inside a dense grid.

Leave this

Three panes at once. On a 1440px canvas that is a library, a workspace and an inspector all competing, and for a four-day split the library is open for about eight seconds of a twenty-minute job. X REP opens the library as a picker instead, and gets the width back.

Everfit — Master Planner, week-by-week view

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

A grid with weeks on the horizontal axis and days stacked vertically. Three view modes: week-by-week shows the same day across several weeks (W1D1, W2D1, W3D1…), day-by-day shows one week’s days, and custom shows a checkbox-selected subset. Drag to reorder at workout, section and exercise level. Copy a workout and paste it into other days, with Shift for multiple pastes. A day header carrying more than one workout shows the count.

Take this

The week-by-week view is the best idea in this entire survey and X REP does not have it. Comparing Week 1 Day 1 against Week 4 Day 1 side by side is how progressive overload is actually judged. Also worth taking: Shift-paste for repeating a day across weeks, and the count badge on a day header.

Leave this

Three view modes on one screen is two more than most trainers will find. X REP should ship week-by-week as a toggle on the builder, not as a third mode with its own dropdown.

Everfit — drag-and-drop as the primary verb

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Reordering and moving happen by dragging, at three levels of nesting.

Take this

Nothing. The capability is right.

Leave this

Drag as the only path. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 requires a single-pointer alternative, and a trainer on a trackpad at a gym desk is not going to enjoy a three-level drag either. X REP keeps drag but ships cut / move to… / paste on every cell’s menu, which is why the builder cell is still marked Beta.

The category as a whole — mobile-first web apps

Partly inferred

What they built

Every product in this category leads with a phone app and treats the web as a wider version of it. That is visible in the shape of the published help screenshots: card lists where a table belongs, one-thing-at-a-time flows, and bottom-sheet-shaped dialogs on a desktop canvas.

Take this

Nothing to take — this is the gap X REP is aiming at.

Leave this

The whole approach. A responsive wrapper around a phone design gives you a roster you cannot sort, a calendar you cannot compare and a ledger you cannot total. X REP’s answer is in the IA: 22 unchanged, 17 reshaped, 10 promoted, 8 merged, 3 deleted.

The category as a whole — money is an afterthought

Partly inferred

What they built

Payments appear as a feature row in every comparison matrix, usually as an add-on with its own price. None of the documentation I could reach presents money as a view — there is no ledger-shaped screen in the published material.

Take this

The gap itself. For a solo trainer in an Indian gym the gym’s cut is the single most consequential number in their month, and no competitor appears to render it at all. X REP puts it in the stat row order (billed → gym’s share → yours → owed), as a ledger column, and in the meter.

Leave this

Treating payments as a billing integration rather than as a screen a trainer opens on the first of the month.

02The three things to actually build

Everything else in this document either confirms a decision already made or names something to keep refusing. These three are real work that does not exist yet.

03What “world class modern” means in 2026, concretely

Six properties, each with where X REP stands. This is the part of the brief worth being specific about, because “modern” used loosely just means “more gradients”.

PropertyWhat it meansWhere X REP stands
Keyboard-first, not keyboard-optionalThe products that feel fast are the ones where a power user never touches the pointer: a palette on ⌘K, J/K down a list, G-then-a-letter to jump.Shipped. Palette, per-screen hint strips, and an 11-row keyboard contract in the design system.
Density as a feature, not a compromise44px rows, tabular figures, sticky headers, a total in the footer. The modern reference for a business tool is a spreadsheet that happens to be beautiful, not a marketing page that happens to hold data.Shipped. Data table at 44px with a tfoot total, and a measured 32px pointer floor.
One accent, used onceThe current house style is a near-monochrome surface set with a single accent that appears two or three times per screen. Rainbow dashboards read as dated in 2026.Shipped, and stricter than most: nine semantic colours and not one invented for the web, plus a rule that lime is a fill and never text on a light ground.

04The one advantage nobody can copy

Where X REP is not competing

Every product in this category is built for a coach with a roster of remote clients and a payment processor. X REP is built for a trainer standing on a gym floor in Adyar whose gym takes 46% of every session they run there and 0% of the ones they run outside it — and who needs to know, on the 1st, what that leaves them. That number does not appear anywhere in any competitor’s published interface. It is the reason the money rules are the least negotiable part of this design system, and the reason the stat row is ordered billed → the gym’s share → yours → owed rather than the other way round.
