Reports & nudges — who is drifting, and one message to all of them

The app can tell you one client’s adherence. Only a desk can rank twenty-two of them, show you the three who are drifting, and let you send the same check-in to all three in one action.

Frames 3 From drawer · 4a 4b 4c 4d 4e 7a 7b 7c Patterns ranked table with inline meters · direction column · bulk bar with the weekly cap · template picker · outbox split

SUPERSEDED · 23 Aug 2026 — the web app is ONLINE-ONLY

This page was written offline-first. That decision has been reversed for the web: the web app writes every response straight to the server — no browser storage, no write queue, no sync. Offline-first remains the architecture of the phone (app/) and of nothing else.

So wherever this page draws or argues for an offline banner, a synced / queued pill, a “held on this device” count, or the sync-queue screen, that chrome is not built on the web. The reasoning around it is kept, because the rest of each finding depends on it — only the offline half is void.

Authority: CLAUDE.md at the repo root, and web app/web/AGENTS.md. Do not re-derive the offline behaviour from this page.

One row per client, sorted by the number that predicts churn. The bar is in the row, not in a chart — a sparkline per client would be prettier and much harder to compare, and comparison is the entire job. The three under 60% are pre-selected, and the header states what they are worth per month, because that is the number that makes a trainer act.

A rate on its own points the wrong way half the time. 70% falling from 90% and 70% climbing from 50% are opposite problems, and the ranking cannot tell them apart — so Trend carries the same comparison the roster figure already makes, per client, against the previous eight weeks. It is one glyph and one number rather than eight more cells, which keeps the column comparable straight down. Colour is rationed to rises and falls of ten points or worse: Ananya at 75% and climbing needs no attention, Vikram at 67% and down eleven does.

The meter column lost its heading. It said “Eight weeks” and showed one aggregate ratio — a label promising a shape it did not draw. The window is already in the sub-head and in the pressed button, and a meter beside the percentage it encodes needs no name of its own.

Every rate here is of what is known, and the banner is what makes that true. Nine sessions in the window were never marked; they are in nobody’s denominator, because a past session nobody closed off is evidence the trainer was on a gym floor, not evidence the client stayed away. One wrong red cell is all it takes for a trainer to stop believing the column, so the screen names the hole and offers to close it. Sneha, at the bottom, has no rate at all — nothing was scheduled, so there is nothing for a percentage to be of, and a dash sorts below a real 42%.

The weekly cap is stated before the send, not discovered after it. Three are selected, one was nudged on Tuesday, so the bar says so and the button says 2. A bulk action whose count changes silently at the moment of sending is the one place this screen could lose a trainer’s trust for good.

Four approved templates, no free-text composer — WhatsApp will not deliver arbitrary text to someone who has not messaged you first, so an open box would be a promise the platform breaks. The preview shows the actual message with the actual free slots merged in.

The delivery column used to claim a read receipt. It showed Delivered and Read; nudge_log.status holds queued, sent and failed, and nothing in the system records a read. Both are now Sent — it left, and that is the whole claim. No number is the fourth state and is deliberately not folded into Failed: a failure is something that went wrong, and this one names its own fix. Arjun’s report was still written and is still right; it is waiting for somebody to add a phone number.

What Meera got, and sending it again

Meera — your week, 17–23 Aug. 4 of 4 sessions kept. 61 sets · 4,820 kg moved. Best: Back squat 90 kg × 5 (was 85 kg × 5). 1 new personal best. Everything the plan asked for. See you this week.

This went out on Sunday night on its own. X REP never sends — the button opens WhatsApp with the text in it and you press send, which is the same refusal every nudge makes.
↑↓walk the weekEntersend againEexport

These are not waiting for approval. The server wrote all twenty on Sunday night and sent them; a queue with an Approve button would be a lie about what already happened. What a desk adds is the outbox — a trainer asked “what did you send me on the 3rd?” in a session cannot answer, and twenty separate screens is twenty navigations. Here the week is one column and any report is one click.

The two that need a hand are the whole reason for the filter. Vikram’s Failed is something that went wrong; Arjun’s No number is something to fix, and his report is written and correct and simply waiting for somebody to add one. Priya’s week was 0 of 3 and still went out, because a blank week is a true week — but her row says read it before you send it again, since a report is the wrong way to open a conversation that needs a phone call.

Sneha R is not in this list at all. Nothing was scheduled for her, so the server had nothing to report on and wrote no row — and a zero-filled report would be a claim nobody made. The same goes for the two paused clients, which is why the sub-head counts twenty and not twenty-two.

The figures here are the stored row the client saw, not a second calculation. A desk that recomputed them would agree today and drift in November when a set from the 3rd gets corrected, and then two people would be holding two screens with two different numbers on them.

04What changed from the phone

On the phoneOn the webBecause
Adherence per clientRanked roster-wide with meters Churn is a comparison problem, and comparison needs rows.
One nudge at a timeThree clients, one send The trainer’s actual intent is “chase everyone who is drifting”.
Weekly reports read one at a timeThe week’s whole outbox in one column They were sent on Sunday without anybody’s help. What a trainer needs is to see what a client received — and to find the two that could not go.
A rate, computed live on the phoneA rate and its direction, ranked The phone has one client on screen and needs the number now. The desk has twenty-two and needs to know which way each is moving.
Nothing needs a signalThe desk assumes a connection Deliberately not carried over. Offline-first is the phone’s argument because a gym floor has no signal; ranking a roster and sending three messages is a job you do sitting down.
The nudge cap stops one sendThe cap is priced into the button One skipped client is a toast. Three of eight silently dropped is a bug report.
