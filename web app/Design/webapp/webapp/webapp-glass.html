Glass — the material for the overlay layer, and nothing else

Yes to glass, and the honest answer is scoped glass: the top bar, the right panel, the modal, the palette and the bulk bar. Not the tables, not the cards, not the rail. Press G to toggle it on any page in this set and judge it by looking — the frame below is a real money ledger with a payment panel over it, which is the hardest case in the product.

The short answer to “can we glass the whole app”

No — and the reasons are specific to this product, not to taste. Published guidance says to avoid glass in four situations, and X REP is in all four at once on its content plane: data-dense text (13.5px tabular figures), nothing behind to blur (the rail sits on the canvas), trust-heavy money contexts (this is a trainer’s income), and cheap devices (visible jank and battery drain on older Android). What is left after removing those four is exactly the overlay layer — and there glass is not decoration, it is the material form of an argument this design system already makes: a payment is decided while looking at the ledger.

The panel is 86% fill at 20px blur with a lit top edge. The table under it is completely opaque — the blur happens in the panel, not in the data. Every control inside the panel sits on a solid fill, which is the rule that stops glass from stacking. The scrim behind dims to 66% but does not blur, so the shape of the ledger stays recognisable.

02Exactly where it applies

Twelve surfaces, five of them glass. The seven solid ones are written into the stylesheet as real declarations rather than as a comment, so a future edit that tries to glass the content plane has to delete a rule that says why not.

SurfaceMaterialSelectorReason
Top barGlass.topContent scrolls under it. There is genuinely something to blur, and the blur is what says “this bar is in front” without a shadow.
Right panelGlass.panelThe panel exists because the ledger behind it matters. Glass is the literal material statement of that argument.
ModalGlass.modalStronger fill (86%) and a wider blur, because a modal must be answered rather than looked past.
Command paletteGlass.palIt floats over an entire screen. The most glass-shaped object here.
Bulk barGlass.bulkReplaces the toolbar over rows that must stay readable — the same argument as the panel. Keeps its lime tint on top of the glass.
Navigation railSolid.railNothing behind it to blur. It sits on the canvas, one step below it. Glass here is GPU cost for an invisible effect.
CardsSolid.cardThe content plane. A card is a surface things are read on, not a sheet that floats over something.
Data tableSolid.tblNever. 13.5px tabular figures over a blurred ground is the exact case every published guide warns against, and these figures are money.
Stat tilesSolid.statFour numbers a trainer checks in one fixation. Translucency buys nothing and costs legibility.
List rowsSolid.lrowThe left pane of a split is content, not overlay.
Set gridSolid.setsThe densest rows in the product, edited mid-session. Absolutely not.
Session blocksSolid.evTheir whole job is to encode floor-versus-remote by colour. Translucency would blend twenty-three of them into each other.

03The seven constraints

01
One sheet per view
Never glass on glass. A modal opening over a panel forces the panel back to a solid fill. Each translucent layer multiplies the opacity loss, and two stacked panes is where legibility collapses.
02
Controls inside glass sit on solid fills
Every field and button inside a panel or modal gets an opaque background. A translucent input on a translucent sheet has no readable state at all.
03
4.5:1 after the blur, in both themes
Measured, not assumed — see the audit in §04. This is only achievable here because the product has no photographs: the ground behind glass is always a near-uniform surface, so contrast is predictable. That is precisely the condition iOS wallpapers violate.
04
prefers-reduced-transparency wins, always
A user who has asked their OS for less transparency gets solid surfaces with no negotiation. Apple shipped a Tinted control in iOS 26.1 specifically because the first release did not respect this strongly enough.
05
backdrop-filter is never animated
The browser recomputes the blur on every frame. Transitions animate opacity and background alpha instead. This is the single biggest performance mistake available with this material.
06
Blur stays between 8px and 26px
8px already buys most of the effect at a fraction of the GPU cost; past ~30px the cost climbs and the look does not improve. X REP uses 20px for panels and the top bar, 26px for modals, 16px for the bulk bar.
07
It is a toggle, not a religion
Press G or the Glass button in the header of any file in this set. Everything falls back to opaque surfaces and the product still works, because the glass carries no information.

One implementation detail worth knowing

Every frame in this design set is drawn at 1440px and scaled with transform: scale() to fit the page. A blur radius scales with its ancestor transform, so at the default 72% zoom a 20px blur renders at about 14px. Press 1:1 in the header to see the real material. This affects the review files only — never the product.

05What this does not do

Not builtWhy not
Specular highlights that track the pointerApple’s version moves light across the glass as the device tilts. On the web that means animating a gradient on every pointer move, over a surface that already costs a blur. It is the first thing to try if this ever runs on a machine that can afford it, and the last thing to ship to a trainer’s five-year-old laptop.
SVG-filter refractionThe technique that gets closest to real glass edges does not work in Safari, and a material that only exists in one browser is not a material.
Glass on the railCovered above: nothing behind it. Apple glasses its sidebars because a wallpaper sits behind them. There is no wallpaper here.
Glass as a light-theme defaultIt works — the tokens exist and the toggle proves it — but the product ships dark-default and light glass over a light canvas has much less to say.
