Schedule — a week that can say half past seven

The first pass drew the week as a matrix of 48px hour cells, and a matrix has one resolution: the hour. All 102 sessions in that file carry the identical inline style top:2px;height:42px — every one exactly sixty minutes, every one exactly on the hour — while the app it documents has stored a per-client session_duration_minutes since schema V2, steps its time field by 15 minutes, and books sessions of 30, 60 and 90. 07:30 appears once in that 73 KB file: as a value you can type into a form, on the same page as a grid that cannot draw it.

This pass throws the cells away. A day is one positioned track at one pixel per minute, so top is the start minute and height is the duration, and the six consequences follow for free: real start times, real lengths, a clash drawn side by side instead of one name on top of another, a now-line, an empty hour that can be priced where it sits, and 494 <div>s replaced by things a keyboard can reach. Not one number in the prose is hand-typed — utilisation, the gap list and its rupee value are computed from the same session list that draws the blocks.

01The audit — eight things, and one of them is the cause of four

Eight things, all of them checkable against the file this page replaces — a 73 KB document about time that could not draw a session starting at half past. The cause is one structural decision, and findings 1 to 4 are the same decision seen from four sides.

01Every session in the file is exactly one hour, and starts exactly on the hour

Defect

What is wrong

The week was a matrix: eight columns by fourteen 48px hour cells, each cell its own containing block, each session absolutely positioned inside one cell. So a session cannot start at 07:30 — the top that would put it there spills into a sibling cell with its own background, its own border and its own hover target — and it cannot run 90 minutes, because there is no height for it. All 102 blocks in that file carry the same inline style. Not similar: identical.

The app has never agreed. TimeField.tsx steps by 15 minutes and its comment names the case — “the one that keeps 07:30 and 06:45 reachable”. 07:30 appears once in the whole schedule file: as a value you can type into the bulk-apply form, on the same page as a grid that cannot draw it.

How to check it

grep -o 'class="ev[^"]*" style="[^"]*"' webapp-schedule.html | sort | uniq -c → 102 top:2px;height:42px
grep -c '07:30' webapp-schedule.html → 1, and it is the <input> in the pattern form.

Defect

What is wrong

The words duration, 45 min and 90 min appear zero times in the file. Defect

What is wrong

webapp-c-domain.html publishes three values for .ev: Height 46px per 60 min, “a 30-minute session is 23px and drops the plan line”, and Min legible 23px — below that, time only. 08Every weekday label in the set was off by one day

Defect

What is wrong

Found by building the month, which is the first view that cannot dodge the question. Every file in this set that draws a week labels its columns Mon 11 … Sun 17 August 2026. In the real 2026 calendar 1 August is a Saturday, so 11 August is a Tuesday — and all seven labels were wrong, by exactly one day, everywhere.

Nothing broke, and that is the interesting part: no view had to agree with any other about which weekday a number was, so the error had nowhere to surface. 02One minute, one pixel

One decision undoes findings 1 to 4: the cells are gone. A day is one positioned track and the hour lines are drawn on it rather than being it — which is how every serious calendar in the category is built, and what the app’s own DayTimeline already does with percentages.

One minute is one pixel

--cw-hour: 60px. It is not a tuning value, it is the geometry: top = startMinute − segmentStart and height = durationMinutes. No scale factor, no rounding, no arithmetic in the spec — a 90-minute session is 90px, and the 15-minute quantum the app steps by is 15px. Every number on this page is the number in the data, which is why none of them is hand-typed: they are computed in gen_schedule.py from the same session list that draws the blocks.

Why 60 and not 48

Because the library’s own floor decides it. Min legible 23px — below that, time only. The shortest session XRep sells is 30 minutes (LENGTHS = [30, 60, 90]). At the old 48px hour a 30-minute block is 24px and loses its client’s name; at 60px it is 30px and keeps it on one line. 60 is the smallest hour that lets the shortest session say who it is — and it happens to be the one that makes the axis arithmetic disappear.

Hour lines at --tx-line-strong, half-hours at --tx-line, and the quarter marks are drawn only while you are dragging. Permanently they are 15px apart and read as hatching, which is noise on a surface whose entire job is to be scanned; on demand they are a ruler. Precision when you are being precise, not precision as decoration.

04The quiet band — and what it may never fold away

The full track is 05:30 to 21:00 — 930px at one pixel per minute, in a 596px viewport. A trainer with a split shift spends 6½ hours of it on hours they are never on the floor, drawn every week, pushing the evening below the fold.

What the band may collapse, and what it may never touch

The rule is narrow on purpose. A span collapses only when it falls outside every day’s working windows and holds nothing. It is not “hide empty hours”: an empty hour inside working hours is the sellable gap this screen exists to price, and folding that away would hide the money to save the pixels. So Wednesday’s empty morning stays at full height and 6½ hours of nobody’s working day becomes one 30px seam — 360px back, 39% of the track, for nothing that was being said.

Both facts come out of working_hours, which is one row per window precisely so that a split shift is two windows — the schema comment says a single range per day “would claim the trainer is free for lunch”. The band is that comment, drawn.

And it refuses to close when something is in it

Working hours constrain what a client can self-book. They have never constrained the trainer — that is the rule WorkingHoursScreen states on its own face, kept from Trainerize. So a 12:30 one-off is a legal booking in the middle of the quiet band, and the band cannot fold it away. It is held open for the whole week, with a reason, rather than closing on six days and lying about the seventh:

05Two clients, one hour

Wednesday has two clients booked over each other: Nikhil 16:30–17:30 and Sneha 17:15–18:15. This is the state the library called “the one that must be noticed”, and the old grid could draw exactly one of the two names.

n overlapping sessions, n columns — and never a stack

FullCalendar’s default is the opposite. slotEventOverlap defaults to true: overlapping events overlap visually, with “at most half of each event” hidden behind the other. That is right for a calendar of meetings, where an overlap is a normal fact you skim past. It is wrong here, because a one-to-one trainer cannot be in two places and an overlap is always an error — and you cannot resolve an error whose second party is behind the first. So the column splits, both blocks keep their name and their hours, and neither is ever occluded.

They also both get the hatch and the warning glyph the library specified and the stylesheet never had. And a bracket appears in the gutter spanning the overlap: a ring is only findable when the thing it rings is on screen, and the gutter is the one column that never scrolls sideways.

Back to back is not a clash

Kavya starts at 18:15, the minute Sneha ends. slotClash() in app/src/clients/conflicts.ts is plain interval overlap with the end exclusive and says so — “so back-to-back sessions are fine” — and the grid now agrees by construction: two blocks that share an edge share no pixel. The old grid could not have disagreed either, because it had no edges to share.

Drawn, and not blocked

The app is careful about this and the web must match it. The diary warns about a double-booking and never blocks, because a session already on the floor is the trainer’s call; the standing-week pickers refuse, because there a clash is not a judgement call but a mistake about to repeat every week for eight weeks. So this screen shows the clash and lets it stand — and §11’s pattern form, which is the other end of that funnel, will not save one. Same fact, two answers, and the difference is whether it is about to be multiplied.

07The month

Month was a button in the toolbar of every frame on this page and it led nowhere — which is the same defect §01 catalogues in the file this one replaces. And it was never an open design question: the app shipped it. app/src/design/MonthGrid.tsx exists, is wired into DiaryScreen, and states the model on itself — “month cells carry dots, never names. A cell is 46px on a 390pt screen and a client’s name in there is a truncated lie — decoration pretending to be data.” This is a port, not an invention, and the two places it departs from the phone are named below.

Why the month must not be this grid

One minute to one pixel is the whole of §02, and it does not survive multiplication: August at that scale is 28,830px of track. So the month is not the week zoomed out. It answers a different question — which weeks are thin, and when am I away — and a different question earns a different encoding. Drawing it as a shrunken week would be the same mistake this page just undid, one zoom level up.

The theory this rests on, and the evidence against the obvious alternative

The obvious alternative is what almost every calendar does: put the sessions in the cell as little chips, and when they do not fit, add “+3 more”. It is worth being precise about why that is wrong here, because it is the default and defaults win arguments by inertia.

Nielsen Norman’s rule for progressive disclosure is a split, and it is stated as a requirement rather than a preference: “you must disclose everything that users frequently need up front, so that they have to progress to the secondary display only on rare occasions.” A +N more link inverts exactly that. What it hides is not the rare advanced case; it is most of a busy day — and a busy day is the thing a trainer opens a month to find. Google Calendar shows two or three events per cell and hides the rest behind that link, and the visible consequence is that most of the top results for “calendar increments” and “see all events month view” in its own support community are people asking how to turn it off. That is a failed split, evidenced by its own users.

A density encoding fails differently, and better. It gives up identity — you cannot see who — and in exchange it is complete: the mark tells the truth about the whole day, at every day, with nothing hidden. Identity is what the Day view is for, and it is one press away. That is two levels, which is also NN/g’s stated ceiling — designs beyond two “typically have low usability because users often get lost when moving between the levels”. Month → Day → the session panel is three surfaces but two disclosures, because the panel is a detail of a day, not a third level of the calendar.

The week

Five weeks, and two of them are the story. The bars make the shape of the month readable before any number is: four weeks at 48%, one at 26% and one hollowed out by the trip. That is the question a month is opened for, and neither the day nor the week view can answer it.

The dated hole finally has somewhere to live. time_blocks has had a table, a schema comment — “one table covers an afternoon and a fortnight away; the only difference is the length” — and no representation anywhere in this design set. It belongs here because this is the only view where it is legible: in a week grid a five-day trip is seven hatched columns with no beginning and no end. So an all-day block spans, with square ends where it continues past the row, and the days under it take the same hatch the week uses for out-of-hours — the same mark meaning the same thing, which is not available rather than merely empty. A part-day block is a notch and not a strip, because it is an afternoon and a strip would claim the day.

And the trip week does not read as a failure. This is the second departure, and it is a correction rather than a port: a blocked day contributes no capacity. Counting the working hours of a day you declared yourself unavailable on makes utilisation punish you for taking a holiday — that week read 3% before the denominator was fixed and reads 21% now, with 5 days away — not counted under it. A metric that shames a trainer for a week they chose is a metric they will learn to ignore.

Wednesday the 12th carries the clash, in the same danger tone and the same glyph the week’s day head uses — because the month is where you notice the one you missed.

The week is the row, and the row gets the week’s own figures

A trainer does not say “the 19th is quiet”, they say “next week is quiet”. The month’s rows are already weeks, so the row is the unit that gets totals: sessions, hours, utilisation and rupees — the same four the week view’s own header carries, which makes pressing one a continuation rather than a context switch. On this month that is the whole insight in five lines: 26% coming back from a break, 21% the week of the trip, and 48% everywhere else.

Whole weeks, including the days outside the month. The first row runs from 27 July and the last into September, and those cells keep their bar and their count — only the date dims. Blanking them would make the first and last rows’ totals wrong, which is a worse lie than a grey number. August’s own total is 65 sessions; the six rows add to more, and they should.

08Booking one — and the nineteen controls that pointed nowhere

The direction of the check is the whole difference

Every product above checks availability when a client books. Not one checks it when the trainer edits. That is backwards for this user: a client booking into a free slot is the system working, and a trainer cutting an hour that already has somebody in it is the moment a mistake gets made — because the trainer is looking at hours and not at names. The app already had this right; conflictingSessions runs per weekday before the save and every day’s hits go into one dialog, “so the trainer answers the whole change once rather than being interrogated once per weekday.” The web’s only job was to stop it being a list.

And one rule this screen must never break

A save that strands nobody goes straight through — no dialog, no confirmation, no are you sure. That is in the app’s header comment and it is the correct reading of Nielsen’s fifth heuristic, which asks for a confirmation before actions “with serious consequences” and not before all of them. A trainer changing their hours in March because the gym changed its timings has done nothing consequential, and a product that stops to ask is a product that has stopped meaning anything when it asks. So the strip in 8b is not furniture: it is absent from 8a, and it is absent from every save that costs nobody a session.

13The keyboard, and the grid that is no longer a grid

The grid stops being a grid, in the ARIA sense, the moment the cells go — and that is a feature. Keep the matrix and a 15-minute resolution means 62 rows × 7 days = 434 cells to arrow through to reach 23 sessions. Nobody does that twice.

KeysWhat it doesWhere the rule comes from
TabEnters the week once, landing on the next session from now — Nikhil’s 17:00 today, not Monday 06:00. APG’s grid pattern: “only one of the focusable elements contained by the grid is included in the page tab sequence”. It is the one property of that pattern worth keeping here.
J K The next and previous session in the day, in clock order — not the next cell. The set’s own list convention, and a day column is a list. webapp-c-data.html, webapp-programs.html and the heuristic audit all specify J/K for moving through rows; the first pass of this page used ↓/↑ instead and gave no reason, which is just an inconsistency.
→ ← The nearest session on the next day, by time of day. Empty day: the day head, so the week is still traversable. Arrows stay spatial. A week is a plane and a list is not, so the two-dimensional move keeps the two-dimensional keys — and ↓ never means two different things on two screens.
↵Opens the session panel. The library’s contract, unchanged.
X … V Lift, move, drop. While lifted, ←/→ change the day and ↑/↓ nudge by 15 minutes — the one place up and down mean earlier and later, because a lifted block has nowhere else to go. Esc puts it back. SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements. The library named it; §09 is the first frame that needs it.
⌘Z Takes back the last move, for as long as its ten seconds are running. The same shortcut and the same promise the builder makes. A move tells the client automatically, so §09 holds the message rather than the regret.
G then D/W/M Day, Week, Month. T alone is today. The rail file’s accelerator ladder, extended into the screen rather than stopping at the rail.

What replaces role="grid"

Each day column is a role="list" of session buttons in clock order, and the seven lists sit in a role="group" labelled with the week. The APG caveat is the reason: “if a cell contains a button and a grid navigation key places focus on the cell instead of the button, screen readers announce the button label but do not tell users a button is present” — and with the cells gone, the block is the button, so focus and the accessible object are the same thing. Each name is the full sentence, with the length in it: “Wednesday 13 August, 17:15 to 18:15, 60 minutes, Sneha R, Legs A, floor, not started, clashes with Nikhil P”. The clash is in the name and not only in the hatch, which is the library’s never-colour-only rule applied to a state it could not previously reach.

What is lost with the cell matrix is the empty cell as a target — you can no longer arrow to 14:45 on Thursday and press ↵. N covers it, and the gap blocks in §03 cover the case that matters, which is the empty time worth selling. An arrow route to every one of 434 empty quarter-hours was never the accessible answer.

14Competitor analysis — eight patterns, and the one the month refuses

The market file examined five patterns and none was the calendar; the rail file examined six and none was a grid. These six are, and they split cleanly: the first three settle the resolution question, the last three are features to refuse or defer. Rows marked Verified quote a vendor’s own documentation. Where a claim is read across vendors rather than quoted, the row says so.

FullCalendar — the grid everyone else is built on

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

The de facto time-grid implementation, and its defaults are the category’s defaults. slotDuration defaults to '00:30:00'; snapDuration “will be whatever slotDuration is”, so drag granularity follows display granularity. slotEventOverlap defaults to true — overlapping events overlap visually with “at most half of each event” hidden.

Take this

The positioned overlay, and snap as a first-class idea. Not one of these options is expressible in a matrix of hour cells; the whole vocabulary presumes a continuous axis. That is the structural fix in §02, and the fact that the reference implementation cannot even describe the old design is the clearest evidence that it was wrong.

Leave this

Both defaults. 30 minutes is not our quantum — TimeField steps by 15 and names 07:30 and 06:45 as the reason — so display and snap are 15 here, with the marks shown only during a drag. And overlap-by-half is refused outright: it is correct for meetings and wrong for a body that can only be in one place. See §05.

ABC Trainerize — six durations, and four layers of availability

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Appointment types carry a duration chosen from 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 and 120 minutes. Availability is four stacked layers — general calendar availability for self-booking, appointment-type availability, date-specific availability, and vacation, which “blocks off the entire day, where you select days rather than hours” — plus a booking window with a minimum notice and a maximum weeks-ahead.

Take this

Duration is a property of the booking, full stop. Six lengths in the direct competitor against one immovable 42px rectangle here is the finding in §01.2 stated from the outside. And the dated hole is real and already in our schema as time_blocks — “one table covers an afternoon and a fortnight away; the only difference is the length” — and drawn nowhere in this design set. §16.

Leave this

The four layers. The app already made this call and wrote it on the screen: WorkingHoursScreen opens by naming Trainerize’s stack and keeping two of it — weekly hours and time blocks — because four “is a complete model and it is far too much for a trainer with a phone”. A wider screen is not a reason to reopen a settled decision; it is a reason for the two we kept to be legible.

Vagaro — the resolution is a setting, and the day is the dense view

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

A salon and spa book with irregular lengths all day, which is the same shape as a trainer’s week. Vagaro’s calendar configuration sets the grid interval to 5, 10 or 15 minutes, and the Day view is the one built for density — up to “seven calendars on phone, 15 on tablet, or 25 on computer” side by side.

Take this

Fifteen is the floor a real booking business needs, from an operator with far more schedule complexity than a personal trainer. It corroborates the app’s own step rather than ours corroborating itself. And Day as the dense layout, not the week zoomed — which is §06.

Leave this

Making it a preference. Three options is right when your customers are a nail bar and a medspa; here it would let the web app and the phone disagree about what a schedule is, and the phone’s answer is already 15. The multi-column day is refused too: those columns are staff, and this product is one trainer. The team feature widens reads and never moves ownership — a teammate’s column here would be the first place that stopped being true.

Everfit — the Master Planner’s three views

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Their release note describes a planner giving “an intuitive, all-in-one weekly, daily, and custom view” of a client’s training calendar: week-by-week grouping the same day across several weeks, day-by-day for one week, and a custom view showing only selected workouts.

Take this

Day earning its own layout. Their day-by-day is not their week at a bigger zoom, and ours is not either — §06 spends the extra width on why each block matters rather than on making an hour taller. Their week-by-week is also a fair idea for §11’s eight-week preview, where the question really is “what does every Friday look like”.

Leave this

The custom view. A saved subset of a calendar is a filter with a name, and this toolbar already has three chips and a view group. Everfit’s planner is also a programming surface — it plans which workouts fall on which day — and ours is a time surface. Merging the two is how a schedule ends up unable to say when anything starts.

Calendly and Acuity — the booking occupies more than its own minutes

Verified from vendor docs

What they built

Both put a gap around an appointment rather than in it. Calendly’s buffers: a 30-minute meeting with 15-minute buffers “needs 60 minutes of free time, though the meeting stays 30 minutes long”, and the connected calendars are checked against the whole window. Acuity calls it padding, before, after or both, and it “doesn’t overlap”.

Take this

The idea is right for a gym floor — a client leaves, the rack gets stripped, the next one arrives, and a trainer who books 06:00 and 07:00 back to back has no minute in between. §09’s read-out therefore names back-to-back instead of calling it “no conflict”: the fact is surfaced even though the rule is not enforced.

Leave this

Shipping the rule, here. slotClash() is ends-exclusive with no buffer, so a web app that padded bookings would reject slots the phone accepts — two halves disagreeing about what a clash is, which is exactly the class of bug the additive-only law exists to prevent. A buffer is a working_hours-level preference and it belongs in Settings, in a commit that changes conflicts.ts. Deferred, and named in §16 rather than smuggled in as a grid behaviour.

Google Calendar — the 30-minute increment nobody wants

Partly inferred

What they built

Its own increment is 30 minutes and not configurable, which generates a steady stream of requests to make it 15 — several of the top community threads for “calendar increments” are exactly that ask. Appointment schedules meanwhile went the other way: Workspace announced slot durations from five to fourteen minutes, where “the previous minimum was fifteen”.

Take this

The now-line and the press-empty-space-to-book affordance, both effectively universal and both absent from the old design (§01.6). And the corroboration is useful: the vendor with the widest calendar deployment on earth is being asked for finer display granularity, not coarser.

Leave this

Its display default. Being the incumbent does not make 30 minutes right for a trainer whose sessions start at 06:15 and 07:15. Also refused: the booking page. Client self-booking is a real feature and it is a product decision behind membership_status and the portal, not a thing a calendar redesign gets to introduce.

Google Calendar — “+3 more”, and the community asking it to stop

Partly inferred

What they built

Its month view puts event chips in the cell and, when they do not fit, replaces the remainder with a +N more link — two or three visible, the rest hidden. The evidence for how that lands is its own support community: the top results for “see all events month view” are a run of threads titled “how to fit more than 3 events in a day”, “how can I expand my month screen… without having to scroll over more” and “can I set my calendar so that ALL events are shown…”. The cap is not configurable.

Take this

The negative result, which is the useful kind. It is the obvious design and it is the one §07 refuses, on Nielsen Norman’s own rule for the split: “you must disclose everything that users frequently need up front, so that they have to progress to the secondary display only on rare occasions.” +N more inverts that — what it hides is not the rare advanced case, it is most of a busy day, which is the thing a trainer opens a month to find.

Leave this

Chips in a month cell, at any count. Truncation is the failure mode either way: three names out of six is a cell that lies, and six names in a 150px cell is six truncated ones. Ours gives up identity deliberately and keeps completeness — the mark tells the truth about the whole day, every day — and identity is one press away in the Day view, which is two levels, NN/g’s stated ceiling.

The category — nobody ships a coach-facing month

Partly inferred

What they built

Everfit’s Master Planner is “weekly, daily, and custom” — there is no month in it — and its client-side visibility is capped at three weeks back and four weeks ahead. Trainerize lets a coach page month-to-month within one client’s calendar. Vagaro and the salon tools do have a month, but their calendars are staff-and-resource calendars for a shop.

Take this

The reason they can skip it, and we cannot. Their calendars are client-scoped: one person’s training plan, where a month is twelve workouts and the answer is obvious. Ours is trainer-scoped — all 22 clients — so a month is {MO_TOTAL} sessions and it answers a question none of their calendars can even ask: which of my weeks is underbooked, and what did that cost. That is the whole argument for building it.

Leave this

Their omission as a reason to omit it. Tempting, and wrong for the same reason the scope differs. But one thing is taken from their restraint: this month has no drag. Vagaro lets you drag in month view; at this scale a drop lands on a day with no time in it, so it would either invent one or ask — and a month is a reading surface. Pressing a day opens the Day view, where a time exists.
