Workout console — the desk half of a floor screen

The page this replaces got its opening argument right and then drew 1 frame. It said so itself: its provenance line lists 13 app frames, and app/src/screens/main/log holds 12 surfaces — 6 screens and 6 sheets — plus the client’s own read-only copy of the same table.

The one frame it drew broke the rule §09 says this screen is most likely to break. It put “Package after this · 7 of 12” in the session summary, and the rule is that finishing the log does not move the pack — a pack moves on done or no-show, never on booked. It dated the session Tue 12 Aug, which is neither today nor a Tuesday. It printed 8 of 12 sets beside 3,420 kg, and those eight sets come to 2,228 — the figure belongs to a finished session. And it labelled the record “her best at this rep range”, which is not what judge computes.

What was missing is the design of a workout log: the 5 verdicts, the plate step that decides whether the client’s phone buzzes, the swap scope that can reach 5 people, the note that is the best idea in the category, and the fact that a record is computed on read — so correcting a set from November fixes nine months of records in the same frame, which is the one thing a desk can do that a phone cannot.

So: 15 frames. Every figure below is derived and asserted from the modules that compute it, down to the half-kilo of incline volume that JavaScript rounds up and Python rounds down.

SUPERSEDED · 23 Aug 2026 — the web app is ONLINE-ONLY

This page was written offline-first. That decision has been reversed for the web: the web app writes every response straight to the server — no browser storage, no write queue, no sync. Offline-first remains the architecture of the phone (app/) and of nothing else.

So wherever this page draws or argues for an offline banner, a synced / queued pill, a “held on this device” count, or the sync-queue screen, that chrome is not built on the web. The reasoning around it is kept, because the rest of each finding depends on it — only the offline half is void.

Authority: CLAUDE.md at the repo root, and web app/web/AGENTS.md. Do not re-derive the offline behaviour from this page.

01The audit — 12 things, and two of them are the app’s own rules

12 things. The first four are arithmetic and a date; the rest are the design of a workout log — four verdicts, a plate, a swap that reaches five people, and a note. Two of them are defects in the app’s own rules rather than in the page: a rule whose type cannot express it, and a field that counts one thing, says another, and is read by nobody.

05“Her best at this rep range” is a different computation

Defect

What is wrong

judge takes today’s top set — heaviest, and on a tie the one with more reps — and compares it against the heaviest load in her whole history on that exercise. Rep range is not in it. A caption that describes a per-rep-range best is describing a feature nobody built, on the one badge whose credibility is the point.

How to check it

const bestLoad = history.reduce((max, s) => Math.max(max, s.loadKg ?? 0), 0). One number, across every earlier set.

02The one screen the web should not win

Nothing here replaces the phone on the gym floor. A trainer is not carrying a laptop to a squat rack, and the 56px thumb targets in the app exist because the thumb is sweaty, the phone is on a rack and nobody is looking at the screen. That page got this right and it is worth keeping verbatim.

What the desk adds is three things the phone cannot do, and every frame below is one of them:

The history beside the entryTwelve weeks of top sets while you type today’s. The reason to look at history is to decide today’s load, and on a 390px screen it is a different screen.
Catching up a session logged on paperFour clients between six and nine, a notebook, and half an hour at nine. A keyboard beats a stepper by an order of magnitude for twelve sets, which is the whole of §12.
Correcting the pastA set typed wrong in November. On a phone you would never find it; on a desk it is two clicks — and because a record is computed on read, fixing it fixes nine months of records in the same frame.

And one thing it must not do: become the place logging happens. The five columns, the Previous column, the tick, the per-exercise rest, the four verdicts — all of it is the phone’s design, ported. Where this console is wider rather than merely different, §04 says so and says why.

01An accelerator is not a new feature

Krause & Harley 2024

“An accelerator is not a new feature — it is merely an additional way of completing an existing action”, and “those users who never discover the accelerator should be able to complete the same task in another way.” This is the rule the old frame failed: it drew a keyboard hint bar and no pointer path for the tick, the note, the swap or the un-tick. Every one of the 6 shortcuts below has a button in the frame beside it. The keyboard is the fast road, not the only road.

02Style them so an expert spots them and a novice ignores them

same

“Style them in a way that differentiates them from the corresponding GUI-command label”, and “accelerators should be readily available, yet easy to ignore.” So they live in a bar at the foot of the frame in mono <kbd>, never in the labels of the buttons they duplicate, and the bar is the last thing in the reading order rather than the first.

03Prioritise what people do repeatedly

same

“Increasing efficiency and productivity really matters only for repeat tasks”, and “learning requires repetition”. A trainer logs 12 sets in a session and four sessions in a morning — about 48 rows before nine o’clock. That is the most repeated action in the entire product, which is why it is the only screen in this set with its own accelerator bar.

04Do not override commonly known shortcuts

same

⌘Z is undo and nothing else. Nothing here rebinds copy, paste, select-all or print — and ⌘↵, which is the one non-obvious binding, is the accept-last-time action, printed in the bar and duplicated as the primary button in frame 1c.

05Provide a safety net for an accidentally triggered accelerator

same, and it conflicts

NN/g offers two nets: an undo, and “a confirmation dialog… serves to prevent unintended actions”. §09 forbids the second outright: undo after, never confirm before, because a trainer logs twenty sets a session and twenty confirmations is a different app. The rule wins, and it is not a fudge — a logged set is reversible, so an undo is a complete net. The one place a confirm survives is discarding a whole session, which is not reversible.

06Recognition rather than recall

heuristic 6

The whole argument for the Previous column being a column. A placeholder inside the load field is recall the moment you start typing; a column beside it is recognition for as long as you need it. Strong ships the placeholder, Hevy ships the column, and §09 protects the column against every future layout pressure by name.

07Visibility of system status

heuristic 1

Three states the old frame had none of: the amber ring on a set this machine wrote, the offline banner naming how many writes are waiting, and the pack figure in the session strip saying unchanged — which is status about something that did not happen, and the harder kind to remember to show.

08Error prevention over error messages

heuristic 5

Nothing on this screen validates a load, because there is no wrong number: 95 kg is a legitimate bench for somebody. So prevention has to work differently — the Previous column puts last week’s number next to the field, which is the only defence available, and §11 accepts that the rest is caught later and makes catching it cheap.

09Match between the system and the real world

heuristic 2

Rest is per exercise. 90 seconds after a bench set and 20 after a curl is one trainer, not two preferences — and every app that made rest a single global number made it a number people turn off. Also the reason the plate step is per-gym: the smallest plate in the room is a fact about a room.

10Flexibility and efficiency of use

heuristic 7

The one heuristic this screen is about. Its real competitor at nine in the morning is a spreadsheet — free, understood, and the fastest set-entry surface ever built. A console that loses to Google Sheets on speed loses, whatever else it offers.

11Aesthetic and minimalist design

heuristic 8

One glow per screen, enforced in CSS rather than left to whoever writes the next screen: the open exercise card gives up its accent the moment a record card appears. If two things glow, nothing is live.

12Vanity metrics

The one thing it borrows outright is the escape. On the phone, backing out of a log is a deliberate act with a confirm on it, because discarding a session is the single exception to undo after, never confirm before. Here it is the same: closing the tab leaves the log open, which is why frame 5a’s first group exists.

15Still open — 6, none blocking

6 things, and none of them blocks. Four are features the mobile spec deliberately skipped or put on a gesture, and the desk is where two of those decisions get weaker rather than stronger. The last one is this page’s own stated purpose, drawn only as a mindset.

01Supersets are out, and the reason is a phone reason

Open

“A superset is a UI for a lifter optimising their own hour, and a trainer supervising one client can simply log two exercises.” That holds on a phone. On a desk, two exercises interleaved across six rows is exactly the shape a grid is good at — and Hevy has had them for years. Deliberately skipped in v1, and the desk is where the argument gets weaker.

02The 3-swap ask has no screen

Open

3 identical swaps and the app asks once whether the program should change. Nothing in this set draws the ask, what it looks like when the trainer declines, or whether declining is remembered — and an ask that returns every third swap is a nag. webapp-programs.html owns it.

03Two logs open at once is supported and undrawn

Open

Frame 5a’s first group exists because a trainer logs four clients a morning. What the console looks like with two live sessions — whether there is a switcher, whether the rail shows both, what the browser tab says — is not decided. The phone answers it with a list; a desk has room for something better and nothing is drawn.

04Reordering exercises

Open

§07 puts it on a long press and a drag of the card, and explicitly refuses a “Reorder” mode reached through a menu. Drag on a desk needs a keyboard alternative under WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 — the schedule file specifies one (X lift, arrows, V drop) and this console has not adopted it.

05The plate step has no setting

Open

2.5 kg here, 1.25 in other gyms, and it decides whether a client’s phone buzzes. plateStepKg is on LogInput and reaches it from somewhere; no screen in this set lets a trainer say which gym they are in. It belongs with the three attention thresholds the clients file also owes to webapp-settings.html.

06Catching up a session from paper has no shape of its own

Open

This page’s own stated reason to exist, and it is drawn as the live console with a different mindset. A trainer entering four sessions from a notebook at nine wants a different arrangement — probably one client after another in one continuous grid, with the date as a field rather than a header. That is a frame, and it is not here.
