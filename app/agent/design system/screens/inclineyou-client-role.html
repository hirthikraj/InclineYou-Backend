Train X · Screens 18–24 · Client role · FR-11

The client role

Until now Train X has been one app with one job — the trainer's. This is the other half: seven undrawn nodes, fourteen frames, and not one new visual idea. Same tokens, same components, same 8pt grid, because the design system is explicit that client home reuses all of it and there is no second design system. Two things change and only two: the tab bar drops its centre button, and the home composition is re-keyed from a business to a single day. The voice is allowed to warm up — the content rules save encouragement for the client side — but it still states facts, which is why there is no emoji and not one exclamation mark in this file. The role is a lens, not an account: same person, same login, same records, read from the other side of the same session.

00 · The bar

Four tabs, no centre button

The trainer's bar is Home · Clients · + · Diary · Money — five slots, because a trainer creates four different kinds of thing between sessions. A client creates one kind of thing, in one place, and the rest of their app is read-only. So the centre FAB comes out and the bar is four labelled tabs: Today · Log · Progress · Payments. Every create action a client has is contextual and lives on the screen that owns it — + in the Progress app bar logs a body metric, Log set lives in the set table, Pay by UPI lives on Payments. A global create button would have to open a sheet with one item in it.

Two IA nodes are deliberately not tabs. Sessions (FR-2.3) is four rows long for a client with a 24-session pack — a tab whose content is four rows is a wasted tab — so it is pushed from Today's pack row, from the section head on a rest day, and from the reschedule push — which is how anybody actually arrives at it. Weekly report (FR-10.2) happens once a week; it arrives as a notification on Sunday and is reachable afterwards from Progress and from the drawer, so it is pushed too. That is the trainer's own rule applied unchanged: four tabs cover the daily surfaces, and nothing daily goes anywhere else.

01 · Today

The counterpart to the trainer's home

The trainer's home is a to-do list — what's next, where the day stands, who needs chasing. The client's Today is the same grammar re-keyed to one person's job: the live card, the stat rail, the band that needs a decision, then the plan. Same components, same order, same reasoning. The hero on the trainer's home and the hero here are the same record read from opposite ends: Ravi's screen says 09:30 Meera Shah, Sneha's says 10:30 Full Body A, and neither is a copy of the other's data — it is the same session row.

What is missing is as deliberate as what is there. No greeting, for the same reason the trainer doesn't get one — it costs the most valuable 60px on the screen. No activity feed: a client's recent activity is their own logging, and that already has a tab. No charts: charts are a Progress thing.

02 · Logging

The same engine, the same table

FR-4.3 is not a client version of workout logging. It is workout logging, opened by a different person. The set table, the previous column, the rest timer, the offline write path and the read-time PR computation are one implementation — which is why a client's log of Sunday's session and Ravi's log of Sunday's session are not two records that sync, they are one record with two readers. Whoever taps the tick first writes it; the other screen shows it.

This is also the one place in the product where the client's voice is allowed to be warmer than the trainer's, because a personal record is the only event in the app worth stopping for. Warmth here is a shorter sentence and a gold circle, not punctuation.

03 · Progress

Charts, each one paired with its number

This is the only client screen that leads with data, and it obeys the system's chart rule without exception: every chart is followed by the figure in text, because a 40px sparkline on a ₹12,000 phone in daylight is a shape, not a reading. Bodyweight uses data-dir="flat" deliberately — the metric component paints down in danger red, and Train X does not have an opinion about which direction a client's weight should move. Green for up and red for down on a bodyweight number is the app taking a position on somebody's body.

Body metrics are append-only (FR-1). A number you logged is never overwritten, so the correction path is another entry, not an edit — and the whole history stays visible to both of you.

04 · Sessions

Four rows, and one that changed

A client's schedule is not a diary. There is no day strip, no week view, no month grid and no agenda gutter — those exist on the trainer's side because a split shift across 27 people needs them. Here it is a pack, then the sessions that are left, in order. The pack card carries the number that actually matters: 4 of 24, and the date the last one falls on.

The client can never move a session themselves. Moving a session changes somebody else's day, so it stays a trainer action, and the client's half of it is one WhatsApp with the new time and a one-tap confirm — which is what .tx-notice is. The sentence that has to be on that card is nothing is deducted for a move: a client who thinks a reschedule cost them a session will ask, and asking is the failure this screen exists to prevent.

05 · Money

A bill, not a book

The trainer's Money tab is a book with two directions — payments in, gym share out, a running balance, a GST line. A client's has one direction, so it is not a ledger; every row is a payment they made, and rendering that with .tx-led[data-dir="out"] would paint every settled receipt in danger red, which in this system means overdue. Receipts are green and tagged Paid. The tab key stays money so the bar matches the trainer's; the label is Payments, because a client has a bill.

UPI Intent, never Collect. Intent runs at 92–95% success against Collect's 6–15 points lower, and NPCI's February 2026 guidance restricts manual VPA entry on mobile anyway — so there is no field to type a VPA into, on either side. Intent falls back to a QR when no UPI app is installed. And the client app never shows Ravi's VPA: his profile deliberately hides it, the deep link carries it, and the client's own UPI app confirms his name before they authorise. Train X never holds, moves or confirms this money, and every screen that could imply otherwise says so instead.

06 · Sunday

The week, and the week with no signal

FR-10.2 is generated on the server on Sunday night and pushed out — the trainer's notification centre already reports weekly report sent to 27 clients. So this is the one screen in the client app that does not come from local data, and it says so: once it lands it is stored and never changes, because a report whose numbers move after you've read it isn't a report.

Everything else is offline-first, unchanged from the trainer's app — local data paints instantly, no skeletons, a per-item Queued tag, and a banner that names what still works. The client's offline banner adds the one thing the trainer's doesn't have to: the two things that genuinely need a signal, so nobody stands in a basement gym tapping Pay by UPI.

08 · The teardown

Ten client apps, and what they cost the client

The pattern across the category is consistent enough to be a design brief: the client app is where the vendor's incentives show. Where the platform sells to trainers, the client app is a compliance instrument. Where it sells to clients, it is a subscription. Almost all of them put chat at the centre — and that is the one convergence Train X refuses, because in India WhatsApp already won and a second inbox is a second place to miss a message.

What India adds

The client on the other end of this app is not the client the category designs for. She may be on a ₹12,000 Android phone — which is why there are no progress photos on the client home, no video autoplay, no chart that needs to be read at 40px, and why every figure is repeated in text beside its bar. She has patchy signal in a basement gym, so logging is local-first and the two operations that genuinely need a connection are named on the screen instead of failing silently.

She may never install the app at all. FR-1 has the trainer creating clients, and the roster explicitly supports invited-but-never-installed — so nothing in the trainer's product may depend on the client's app existing. Sessions, packs, payments and receipts all work with one participant on a phone. That single constraint is what disqualifies most of the table above: Trainerize, Everfit and Kahunas all assume the client signs up, and in this market a meaningful share never will.

She pays by UPI to a personal VPA, or cash on a gym floor. There is no card, no mandate, no wallet and no platform balance, which is why the client app has no payment history that Train X owns — only receipts her trainer issued. And she talks to her trainer on WhatsApp, already, today, about everything. So Train X ships no chat: every message button in these fourteen frames opens a pre-filled WhatsApp draft and sends nothing silently. An app that adds a second inbox to that relationship has added a place for a message to be missed.
