Train X · Screen 04 · Clients · FR-1 · FR-5

Clients

Fourteen states and a tap map for every clickable thing on the screen, built on a teardown of how nine coaching platforms organise a roster. They agree on one thing and miss one thing. They all agree the list has to surface who needs you — Trainerize computes it, Everfit segments it, TrueCoach sorts by it. And every single one of them puts the money on another screen. For a trainer selling ten-session packs for cash and UPI, who owes you is the roster.

01 · The roster

One list, sorted by who needs you

The default order is not alphabetical. Alphabetical is a lookup order, and lookup is what search is for — a trainer opening this tab is far more often asking “who do I have to chase?” than “where is Ananya?”. So the list opens on Needs attention, pinned above everyone else, and A–Z is one tap away.

03 · Acting on a client

Three ways in, and none of them is the only way

NN/g's finding on contextual swipe is blunt: “burying key actions behind a contextual swipe prevents users from discovering them”. So the rule here is that swipe is never the only path. Every action on the swipe is also in the long-press menu, and everything in the long-press menu is also on the client's own screen.

What everyone got right

Every serious platform computes who needs attention rather than asking the trainer to remember. Trainerize's auto-tags are the best version of this idea in the category.

What everyone got wrong

Money is on another screen in all nine. For a trainer selling packs for cash and UPI, “who owes me” is not a report — it is the roster, and it is the first thing they scan.

What we deliberately skipped

No compliance percentage on the row. A number like 68% reads as a grade and starts arguments; “no workout in 9 days” is the same fact and it names the action.

What India adds

Batches, not groups. UPI and cash side by side. And WhatsApp as the messaging layer — one chat per client, never a broadcast, because a group message about money loses clients.

08 · Adding a client

Two fields, and the money question on its own screen

The empty state in 4a already made the promise — a client needs a name and a phone number — so the form has to keep it. Everything a competitor asks for at intake (goals, injuries, measurements, a program, a package) is a row you can fill in later from the file, and none of it blocks the first session. The one thing that does earn a second screen is money: floor or remote, who collects, and what the gym takes. Putting that under the two required fields would make the required fields look optional, so it gets a step of its own — pre-filled from Settings, and skippable in one tap.

09 · The client file

One file, four tabs, and the money screen that already exists

This is the screen every “tap a client” in the app has been pointing at. It is one record with four views — overview, programs, sessions, package — and the ledger drawn on the Money screen as Farhan's book sits one tap inside the Package tab rather than becoming a fifth tab. Four already scroll horizontally at 360dp; the design system's own tab demo exists because four overflowed. Like that ledger, the file is a pushed screen with no bottom bar — a back arrow returns you to the roster you came from, which is where a trainer's attention lives. And the rule for the overview is the one the competitors break: every figure on it is a link to the screen that owns it. A number a trainer can't tap is a number they have to leave the file to chase.

10 · Editing, pausing, ending

Three ways out, and only one of them is a delete

4b already drew the difference between paused and archived and said what it costs: paused keeps the pack, archived closes it. This is where that decision is actually made, next to the third option nobody should reach for. Edit is the boring screen and it matters most: a form that silently refuses an edit teaches a trainer that the app is broken, so every locked field carries the reason it is locked, on the row, in a sentence.

11 · Body metrics

Append-only, because a record you can edit is not a record

Weight and waist, and one decision that separates this screen from every competitor's: a saved measurement cannot be edited or deleted. Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit and Hevy all let a trainer change a past reading, and it is the wrong call for the one screen whose whole job is to be believed — a client who has watched their weight chart change shape after the fact will never trust it again. A wrong number is corrected by appending the right one, and the history says so on the row.

What Everfit and TrueCoach got right

An overview made of figures, and metrics with their own chartable history. Everfit naming its first tab Overview is a small thing that fixes a big one — you land on where the client stands, not on what they did last Tuesday.

What all nine got wrong

Two failures, and they pull in opposite directions. The coaching apps require a client account before the file means anything — Trainerize tags the ones who haven't signed up, Everfit calls them “waiting activation”, Hevy Coach can't work without it. The booking apps have no workouts at all. Nobody ships a file that is complete for a client who trains three times a week and has never installed anything.

What we deliberately skipped

No progress photos, no nutrition tab, no forms, no goals module, no notes-with-attachments. Photos are the one thing on this list a trainer must never store casually on a phone that gets handed around a gym floor, and the other four are tabs that exist so a sales page can list them. Four tabs, and metrics is a push off the overview — because four tabs already scroll at 360dp, which is why .tx-tabs scrolls at all.
