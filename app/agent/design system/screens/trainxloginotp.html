05 · Resolve role

Only shown to people who are both

Your map says a person can be a trainer and a client. That's a real case — most independent coaches also train. For everyone else this screen never appears; the app resolves the single role silently and goes straight to the Deck.

5a · Both roles
9:25

Welcome back, Ravi

You coach clients here and you train here. Where do you want to start?

Switch any time from your avatar. We'll open here next time.

Each card carries live proof it's the right one — client count, current programme. A role picker with two bare labels makes people guess.

Behaviour

Skip it whenever you can

One role → resolve silently. This screen only earns its place for the genuinely dual user, and even then only on first launch and after an explicit switch.

The role is a lens, not an account

Same person, same login, same data. Switching changes the tab bar and the home composition — never the identity or the sync state.

Remember the last choice

Store it locally, not on the server. A trainer who opens at 6am to coach and at 8pm to lift shouldn't re-pick every time; opening where they left off is right far more often than asking.

Client attributes

type="text" · inputmode="numeric" · autocomplete="one-time-code" · pattern="\d{6}". iOS also needs textContentType = .oneTimeCode; Android needs SMS Retriever plus the User Consent API as a fallback. One real input under six slots — never six inputs.

Never do this

Ask for the code through any other channel. The trust line on the OTP screen is not decoration — OTP-relay fraud over phone calls is common in India, and the app should say plainly that it will never ask.

Accessibility

The headline is the field's label via aria-labelledby. Errors sit in an always-present aria-live="polite" slot. The countdown is not announced on every tick — only at 30s, 10s and 0.

Instrument the funnel

There is no trustworthy published OTP delivery rate for India, so measure your own: sent → delivered → entered → verified, split by operator and by handset. DLT error codes 633 / 635 / 5101–5108 are your earliest warning that a template edit broke sign-in.

07 · Identity — the gap that would have shipped

What happens when the number isn't known

In your model, trainers create clients (FR-1). So the most likely first experience for a client is: download the app, type their number, and the system has never seen it — because their trainer hasn't added them yet, or added a different number. Without this screen they are simply stuck at a spinner, and that is the single most common way a coaching app loses a client on day one.

08 · When the SMS doesn't arrive

Budget for a 3–8% hard-fail tail

There is no credible published OTP delivery figure for India — every "99.9%" you'll read is vendor marketing. The honest planning assumption is 5–15s at p50, 30–60s+ at p95, and a few percent that never land at all. So the OTP can never be the only path.

09 · Sessions across devices

Where offline-first and auth can destroy data

This is the most dangerous interaction in the whole app and it is invisible on a screen. A trainer logs a full session in a basement with no signal. That evening they sign in on a new phone. If signing in on the new device revokes the old session, the old phone hits a 401, a naive logout handler wipes local storage — and an entire session's sets are gone, silently, with no error the user ever sees.

The rule

Never wipe local data on a 401. Token revocation is a sync-permission event, not a data-lifecycle event. Quarantine the outbox and surface it — never delete it.

Allow concurrent sessions

Single-session logins are actively harmful here. OWASP leaves it to the application; the old ASVS "no duplicate sessions" rule was a Level 3 requirement and was dropped. Let both devices live.

Notify, don't interrupt

New-device sign-in completes normally. The old devices get a push with a one-tap "that wasn't me". Interstitial warnings are reserved for new device plus new country.

Drain before you revoke

Remote sign-out forces a final sync push first. If it can't, the confirm says "3 workouts still to upload from that phone" instead of quietly discarding them.

Update to keep going

A hard wall only for security or protocol breaks; everything else is a dismissible prompt. Always leave a support route on the wall — a screen with exactly one button and no escape is what gets an iOS build rejected under Guideline 2.1.

Platform notes

Android

Play In-App Updates (com.google.android.play:app-update). Priority 4–5 → IMMEDIATE, 2–3 → FLEXIBLE after staleness days. IMMEDIATE is still cancellable, so you need your own server-side minimum-version floor as well. Priority is set at release time and can't be changed after publishing.

iOS

No Apple API — it's a server floor plus your own screen, deep-linking to itms-apps://. Exempt the build currently in review, or a reviewer sees only the wall and rejects under 2.1 / 4.2.

Keep a remote kill switch

The floor must be lowerable without shipping a build. Wall your whole user base by accident and the only fix is a release you can't ship because they can't reach it.

11 · Corrections to the spec I gave you

Two things in the first version were wrong

1 · The SMS format

I specified @trainx.app #481234 as the last line. That does nothing on native iOS or Android. It's the WICG origin-bound-code spec, used by WebOTP in browsers — not by native apps. Android's SMS Retriever needs an 11-character app hash and a ≤140-byte message; iOS just needs textContentType = .oneTimeCode and parses heuristically. Worse, on Indian DLT that line is an unclassified CTA-shaped string that may trip URL/CTA whitelisting scrubbers — a delivery risk that buys nothing.

2 · The consent line

"By continuing you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy" is bundled implied consent, which DPDP s.6(1) reads down. It's replaced with a purpose-specific line, and real consent moves to the profile step where body metrics are actually collected.

RegisterTemplate body
Android481234 is your Train X code. Valid 10 min. Do not share.
 
FA+9qCX9VSu  ← 11-char app hash · whole message ≤ 140 bytes
iOS481234 is your Train X code. Valid 10 min. Do not share.  ← no hash line
DLT variableone variable, tagged {#numeric#} under the Jan-2026 tagging rules · ≤30 chars · static content ≥60% of the body
FallbackAndroid SMS User Consent API behind SMS Retriever — needs no hash and no format control, so it still works when a template edit silently breaks the hash line

Why two templates

The hash line is Android-only and eats bytes you don't have to spare against DLT's mandatory brand-name-in-body rule. Register both under the same header and pick server-side — the client already tells you the platform.

What will actually break delivery

Carrier scrubbing is an exact, case-sensitive match against the registered template. A curly quote pasted from a doc, a double space, or a variable over 30 characters and the message is rejected — errors 633, 635, 5101–5108. Alert on those in production; they're your earliest signal a template edit broke sign-in.

The existential one

Under the 2025 amendment, a repeat complaint violation is one year's disconnection of all telecom resources across every operator. A marketing team misusing your transactional header can kill OTP delivery for a year. Keep promotional and transactional headers strictly separate.

DPDP timing

Consent and notice obligations commence ~14 May 2027, so there's no live penalty exposure today — but retrofitting granular consent into a shipped auth flow is far more expensive than building it now. The sharper risk is children's data: under-18 needs verifiable parental consent and no behavioural tracking. Add a date-of-birth gate at profile setup.
