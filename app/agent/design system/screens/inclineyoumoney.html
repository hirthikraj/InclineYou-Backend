Train X · Screen 06 · Money · FR-6

Money

Sixteen states and a tap map for every clickable thing on the screen. Every coaching platform builds this screen as a payment processor — Stripe balance, payouts, failed charges. Train X can't, and shouldn't: the money arrives as cash in a gym, UPI straight into the trainer's own bank, or at the gym's counter. So this is a book, not a dashboard, and the right thing to copy is not Stripe. It's OkCredit and Khatabook — the digital bahi khata that ten million Indian shopkeepers already understand.

01 · The month

Two numbers, and whose money it actually is

Collected and still-owed, side by side, because those are the only two figures a trainer checks. Then the line no western app has: your share. Indian gyms take 50–70% of personal-training fees — trainers keep “30% to 50%” — so a screen that shows ₹1,06,500 collected and stops there is telling a trainer a number that isn't theirs.

06 · When it never arrives

Write off, don't delete

Some money doesn't come. Every ledger app lets you delete the entry, which hides it and quietly rewrites the client's pack. A write-off does neither: it leaves still owed, keeps the row in the client's book, and lands in the year's written off total — so in March the number you look at is the truth.

07 · The teardown

Nine products, and what each one thinks a money screen is

What OkCredit got right

A ledger reads like a chat, because a chat is the one interface every Indian phone user already knows. Chronological, two-directional, with a balance you can point at. Ten million shopkeepers is not a design opinion, it's evidence.

What the coaching apps got wrong

All of them assume the money flows through the software. In India it flows through UPI to a personal VPA, cash on a gym floor, and the gym's own counter — so a Stripe balance is a screen about money that doesn't exist.

What we deliberately skipped

No in-app payments, no payouts, no card storage. Taking money means a PSP licence, KYC and holding other people's funds. Train X keeps the book and stays out of the flow — and says so on the empty state.

What India adds

The gym cut — trainers keep 30–50% of PT fees, so “your share” is the real number. The ₹20 lakh GST line for services. Cash as a first-class method. And WhatsApp as the only collection channel that gets read.

08 · Rules this screen sets

Things a dev can hold us to

RuleWhy
Train X never holds, moves or confirms moneyNo PSP licence, no settlement account, no liability. Every screen that could imply otherwise says so instead
A payment is recorded by the trainer, never auto-detectedWe cannot read their bank. Auto-marking from an SMS parse would be wrong often enough to destroy trust in the book
Money in is green with a down arrow; money out is red with an up arrowThe OkCredit convention. Ten million people already read it this way — inverting it to match a western accounting app would be a self-inflicted wound
Every ledger entry is editable and deletable, and delete always asksIt is a book, and books get corrected. But a deleted payment changes a pack, so it is never silent
Receipt numbers are issued locally from a device-scoped rangeFR-8. Cash arrives in basements. Two devices must never mint the same number
The gym percentage is applied at record time and stored on the entryIf the contract changes in October, September's split must not move
Amounts are integer paise, never floats₹0.01 of drift in a ledger is the end of the ledger
Every figure is tabular-nums and right-aligned in a columnColumns of money that don't line up cannot be scanned, and scanning is the whole job
Write-off is a first-class action, not a deleteSome money never arrives. Deleting it hides it; writing it off keeps the history and shows the year's real total
The GST warning fires a month before the line, not afterLate registration carries a penalty. A warning after the fact is just bad news
